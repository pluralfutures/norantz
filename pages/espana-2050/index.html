Force when published: advisory Status today: not yet researched Reviewed as of 16 Nov 2026 6 of 9 themes · 38 of 102 items reviewed Next review: not set EN

In one line

Spain has grown faster than both the report's paths by adding workers, but the productivity gap the report put at its centre has not narrowed.

At a glance

España 2050 was written by the Spanish government's foresight office in May 2021 as the basis for a long-term national strategy. It aimed to close Spain's gap with eight leading EU economies by 2050. It set a path of reform against a baseline without it, and assumed slow population growth and a calm world outside. Five years on, Spain is on neither path. The economy grew between 2.4% and 3.7% a year in 2023–2025, faster than both, mostly by adding workers through migration. Output per hour is still about 74% of the benchmark level. The proposals on productivity, education, pensions, and care still stand; those on migration, tax, and housing are worth revisiting.

Three things to know

  1. Spain had 49.8 million residents on 1 July 2026, already above the 49.3 million the report used for 2050. As of Aug 2026 · INE
  2. Output per hour has stayed at about 74% of the eight benchmark countries' level for 18 years. The gap the report put at its centre is still there. As of Oct 2026 · BBVA Research [GAP] Tier 2 source; verify at INE or Eurostat
  3. House prices rose 12.2% in the year to the second quarter of 2026. The report expected them to rise in line with inflation. As of Sep 2026 · INE

02 · How to read this page

A vision with a comparison path: the goal is an aim, not a forecast

This report is a vision with a comparison scenario. Its convergence path is the authors' goal for 2050. Its baseline projects the trends of 1980–2019 forward, to show what happens without reform. Neither is a forecast.

Where a figure is "behind", that describes the distance between the latest published number and the report's own target. It does not describe a government. The yardstick throughout is the report's own aims, not the reviewer's view of what is good.

Two readings are kept apart. A rating says what the evidence shows so far about something the report assumed. A pressure reading says what new conditions may mean from here. This page has no pressure readings yet, because no change scan has been run.

Where lower is better (energy poverty, housing cost overburden), "ahead" still sits above the line.

This report gives figures for both paths and dates most of its targets, so we compare them with published statistics. Ratings rest on official sources only: statistics offices, central banks, ministries, and international bodies. Think tanks give context. News reports never support a rating.

Part B · The report, then

03 · Why it was written

Written during the pandemic, it set out to close Spain's gap with Europe's leaders
  • Who wrote it. The Oficina Nacional de Prospectiva y Estrategia, the government's foresight office. The report draws on about 1,650 publications and 500 data series.
  • The moment. Written during the COVID-19 pandemic, which the report treats as a passing shock with recovery by 2022–2023. European recovery funds were about to arrive, and the report counts on them.
  • How it was made. The 27 EU states were sorted into low, medium and high performers. Spain's past 30 to 40 years were reviewed and projected to 2050 as a baseline. A second path was drawn in which reforms close the gap with the high group. A roadmap of 50 targets and more than 200 proposals works back from that path.

[GAP] Who asked for it, who was consulted, and the number and names of contributors are not recorded. [STRAIN] "The moment" is assembled from two register items; the pilot has no account of the political moment.

08 · Where things stand

Spain is on neither path: faster growth from more workers, not higher productivity

Spain has grown faster than both paths by adding workers, mainly through migration, while output per hour has barely moved. Jobs and income are moving toward the convergence path. Productivity, the variable the report made central, is not. The report did not model this path.

11 · What the report did not cover

The report does not discuss defence, tariffs, natural gas, or semiconductors

A word search of the main text, for topics that have since mattered and were already in view in 2021.

  • Not discussed: defence spending, tariffs, protectionism, strategic autonomy, natural gas, semiconductors, and disinformation.
  • Mentioned in passing: inflation four times; interest rates, public debt, and geopolitics once each.
  • Named as open questions, not modelled: a rift between China and the United States, regional supply chains, and a stronger or weaker EU.

Developments after publication are in §10, not here. The word search also found no mention of generative AI.

[TRIAL] Which topics were "already in view in 2021" is a new judgement. Generative AI is left off the list on that basis; the pilot does not date when it became a policy topic.

15 · How this review was done

AI read the report and gathered evidence; no rating has been reviewed by a person
  • Track. A vision, with the baseline as a comparison path.
  • What the AI did. Read the report and logged 102 items. Ran a word search for absent topics. Grouped the items into nine themes and ranked them by how many chapters depend on each. Gathered evidence for six themes and proposed ratings.
  • What people checked. One reviewer chose which themes to test first and set the source rule. No rating has yet been reviewed by a person.
  • Sources used. Official: INE, Banco de España, AIReF, Eurostat, the European Commission, OECD, IMF, and Spanish ministries. Research: BBVA Research, CaixaBank Research, Fedea, Funcas, and Real Instituto Elcano.
  • Not reviewed. Climate timing, the eight-country benchmark, and political delivery. 64 of 102 items have no rating. No change scan and no commitment pass.
