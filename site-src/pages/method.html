How a review is done

Each review reads a report on its own terms first, then checks what it assumed and aimed for against the evidence since. The same steps run for every report, so two reviews can be compared.

A vision and a scenario set are read differently

A vision describes one desired future and a route to it. Its goals are the authors' aims, not forecasts. The review asks whether what the vision assumed has held, and where things stand against its own path.

A scenario set describes several possible futures and does not choose one. The review asks which forces the scenarios turned on, and which of the futures the evidence now resembles, element by element. No scenario is named as "the" outcome unless most elements agree.

Some reports do both: they set out several futures and argue for one. España 2050 is this kind. Its preferred future is read as a vision, and the others as comparison paths.

Nine steps, each with a named human check

StepWhat the AI doesWhat a person checks
1. IntakeRecords what the report is, who published it, and whether it is still in force.The classification and status
2. RegisterLogs every claim, goal, and commitment with its page reference.Assumptions the report implies but does not state
3. ConsistencyFlags places where the report's own figures disagree.Which figure to test against
4. ThemesGroups items into themes and ranks the assumptions most goals rest on.Which themes to review, in what order
5. EvidenceGathers the latest official figures, dated and sourced.—
6. What has changedLists major shifts, trends, and emerging issues since publication, and what each bears on.The list, and the direction of each link
7. ReadingsApplies the labels below and tests the rate each target now needs.Every reading, above all "not supported so far" and "behind"
8. FindingsWrites the findings, each in four beats: intent, then, now, so what.The page, before it is published
9. LimitsLists unverified figures, unopened sources, and every proxy the reviewer made.—

Scenario sets follow eight steps of the same shape (method stages E1 to E8).

Readings rest on official sources only

SourceExamplesUse
Tier 1Statistics offices, central banks, finance ministries, IMF, World Bank, WTO, ILO, APOSupports a reading
Tier 2Think tanks and research institutesContext and trends; emerging issues
MediaNews reportsNever supports a reading. May flag a candidate or carry an official figure, which is then verified at source.

Every figure carries an "as of" date and a link. Where a plan's 2025 "baseline" was a projection, the published 2025 outturn replaces it, and both are shown.

What each label means

Colour tells time: Loam is what the report said, then; Sky is what the evidence shows, now. Shape tells status. Dotted lines are planned; solid lines are observed. There is no overall score.

How a claim has fared so far
HoldsThe evidence matches what the report expected.
Goes furtherThings moved further in the direction the report expected.
Holds in partThe direction holds; the size or timing does not.
Not supported so farThe evidence so far runs against what the report expected.
Not yet reviewedToo early to test, or not yet checked.
Where a target figure stands against the report's own path
AheadBetter than the path for its year.
On pathClose to the path.
BehindBelow the path. This describes a distance from the plan, not a government.
MetThe target has been reached.
No longer in forceThe target belongs to a report since replaced; its last-known status is shown.
What new conditions may mean from here
Under pressureNew conditions push against the claim.
SupportedNew conditions run with it.
MixedBoth at once.
WatchSomething new may bear on it; not yet clear which way.
UntouchedNothing found that bears on it.
Recommendations
Still standsThe reasoning behind it still holds.
Worth revisitingAn assumption it rests on has moved.
Revisit: new conditionsSomething new since publication bears on it.
