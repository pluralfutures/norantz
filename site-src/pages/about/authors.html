About · Right of reply

If you wrote or published a reviewed report

You know your report better than any reviewer. Every review page has a place for your response, and we want to hear where our reading falls short. A review is meant to start a conversation with you, not to end one.

What a review of your report is, and is not

A Norantz review reads your report on its own terms, then sets what it assumed and aimed for beside the latest official figures and the changes since it was published. It is drafted with generative AI and checked by people. It may contain errors.

It does not grade your report, your institution, or any government. It does not say whether your report was right or wrong. It says where things stand at a date, and which parts of the report may be worth discussing again. Many of the questions it raises are ones only the authors can answer.

Before publication: you see the draft first

Before a review is first published, we write to the publisher of the report, and to any named lead authors we can reach. We send:

  • the draft review page;
  • the register of claims, goals, and commitments logged from your report, with page references;
  • the list of sources and figures behind each reading.

You have 15 working days to comment. We check every factual point you raise before publication. You do not need to approve the review, and we do not need your approval to publish it, but a reply received in time is published with the review from the first day.

We also offer a conversation, by call or in person, with the person responsible for the review.

After publication: reply at any time

You can send a response at any time after publication. We publish it in full in the "Response from the authors" section of the review page, with its date and your name and role, within 10 working days of receiving it.

  • Length: up to 600 words, plus links. Longer replies are linked in full.
  • Language: English or the report's own language. We add a translation, labelled as ours.
  • Edits: none, except to remove anything unlawful or personal data about third parties. If we must remove something, we say so in the reply.
  • Our answer: we may add a short note under your reply saying what we changed or why we did not. We do not argue with you on the page.

Factual errors are corrected; differences of reading are shown side by side

You tell usWhat happens
A figure, quote, page reference, or date is wrongWe check it and correct the page. The correction is dated on the page and listed in the changelog.
The review misstates what the report meantWe re-read the passage. If you are right, we correct the page. If we still read it differently, your reply sits beside our reading.
A better official source existsWe check it against our source rules. If it qualifies, we use it and say so.
The report has been revised, superseded, or adopted into law sinceWe update the report's standing and, where needed, the scope of the review.
You disagree with a readingA reading changes only on evidence. Your reply is published either way.

We would like to work with you

The reviews are most useful when they lead to a better-informed discussion. Authors and publishers can:

  • point us to data, later plans, or progress reports we have missed;
  • propose indicators for goals that have no number;
  • suggest the questions a review should ask at its next update;
  • invite us to a seminar or workshop where the review is discussed.
