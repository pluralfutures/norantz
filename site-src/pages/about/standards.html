About · Editorial standards

Read generously. Check rigorously.

The rules every review follows, whichever country and whichever government wrote the report. They exist so that a review can be trusted as a fair starting point, and challenged where it falls short.

Version 0.1 · 18 Nov 2026 · Draft for review

1. Reviews open a discussion; they do not close one

Norantz reviews are written to widen public conversation about national futures, and to give a sourced starting point for deeper, human-led discussion and analysis. They are drafted with generative AI and may contain errors. We say so on every review page, and we design every page so that a reader can check each claim in one step.

We welcome use of the reviews in classrooms, workshops, newsrooms, parliaments, and ministries, as long as they are quoted fairly (see press) and read for what they are: a dated reading, not a final judgement.

2. The authors' intent comes before what has changed

A national vision is a serious piece of work, written under real constraints. Every finding follows the same four beats:

  1. Intent: what the authors set out to do.
  2. Then: what the report said, with its page reference.
  3. Now: what the evidence shows, dated and sourced.
  4. So what: what follows for the report's own goals.

A report is read on its own terms. A vision's goals are its authors' aims, not forecasts, and a scenario set is not judged on whether one scenario "came true". Whether the report foresaw a later change is recorded as a fact, never as a criticism.

3. We describe; we do not grade

Nothing on this site scores, grades, or ranks a government, an author, or a report. There is no overall score, no percentage, and no league table. Status is shown by a shape and a word, never by red, amber, or green.

Figures come before labels: "11.3% in 2025 against a path of 9.8%: behind", never "Behind: 11.3%". A gap between a plan and the data describes a distance, not its cause. We do not assign blame.

4. Readings rest on official evidence, dated and linked

  • Readings rest only on official sources: statistics offices, central banks, finance ministries, and international bodies such as the IMF, World Bank, WTO, ILO, and APO.
  • Think tanks and research institutes give context and trends. News reports never support a reading.
  • Every figure carries an "as of" date and a link. Figures read off a chart are marked "~" and never rated.
  • Where a figure was taken from a second-hand account, it is marked "verify at source" until checked.
  • Every review lists its own limits: what could not be checked, and what the reviewer had to assume.

The full rules are on the method page.

5. Generative AI is disclosed, and people stay accountable

Reviews are drafted with generative AI, in the same steps for every report. We disclose this on every review page, together with what the AI did and what people checked, step by step.

  • Every review names the person responsible for its human checks.
  • People review every reading before publication, above all "not supported so far" and "behind".
  • Where a check has not been done, the page says so. It never implies more human review than took place.
  • AI is not used to imitate a real person, to invent quotes, or to create images of real people.
  • Responsibility for what is published rests with Plural Futures, not with the AI.

[TRIAL] Proposed rule: no review is published until a named person has signed off its readings and its page. Neither pilot page meets this yet.

6. The same rules apply to every country and every government

  • Norantz takes no side in party politics. A review does not endorse or oppose a government, a party, or a policy.
  • Every report is reviewed with the same method and the same source rules, whoever published it.
  • Which reports are reviewed, and in what order, follows published criteria (see funding and selection).
  • No photographs of politicians or officials appear on the site. People are named only as a report's authors or publishers, or where they ask to be named in a reply.

7. Elections: no new reviews in the final weeks of a national campaign

Reviews of a national plan can be drawn into an election campaign. To avoid that:

  • We do not publish a first review, or a new version that changes readings, of a country's report in the six weeks before a national election in that country.
  • Corrections of factual errors continue at all times.
  • We do not time a publication to any political event.

[TRIAL] Proposed default for the open election policy (site architecture open item 5). The six-week window is a placeholder; it decides the España 2050 launch date.

8. Funders and partners do not shape reviews

  • No funder, partner, or client sees a review before publication, or has any say over its content.
  • We do not review a report whose authors or publishers fund Norantz, or are clients of Plural Futures on related work, without declaring it at the top of the page.
  • Anyone who works on a review declares any link to the report, its authors, or its publisher. A declared link is shown on the page; a close link means that person does not work on it.

[TRIAL] Proposed conflict-of-interest rules. Funding policy itself is open (open questions §2, item 4).

9. Reports are read in their own language

Reports are read in the language they were published in. Key quotes are given in the original beside the English. Each review is published in English and, where we can, in the report's own language. Any machine translation is labelled as such until a fluent speaker has checked it.

10. Errors are corrected openly, and authors can always reply

When we get something wrong, we correct it on the page, say what changed and when, and list it in the corrections and changelog. Earlier versions stay at their own addresses. The authors and publishers of every reviewed report can reply on the page itself (see for report authors).

11. Complaints are answered within 20 working days

If you think a review breaks these standards, write to us and say which standard and which part of the page. We acknowledge every complaint within 5 working days and give a reasoned answer within 20. If you are not satisfied, you can ask for the complaint to be reviewed by a director of Plural Futures who did not work on the review.

12. These standards are reviewed every year

We review these standards at least once a year, and after any serious complaint. Changes are dated and listed in the changelog.
