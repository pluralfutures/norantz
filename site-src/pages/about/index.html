About Norantz

A second reading of national futures

Governments publish long-range visions and scenarios to steer a country for decades. Few are read again once the decade they describe arrives. Norantz reads them again.

What a review asks

For each report, Norantz asks four questions. Which of its assumptions have held? Where do things stand against its own goals? What has changed since it was published? Which of its recommendations are worth revisiting?

A review states what the authors set out to do before it says what has changed. It describes; it does not grade. Nothing on this site scores a government, an author, or a report.

The name is a question

Norantz is Basque for "towards where?", from nora (where to) and -ntz (towards). The name keeps the question open. Each review is a reading at a date, not a final word.

Who it is for

Anyone who wants to know what a national plan promised and where things stand, in a few minutes. Advisers who need to know what is new and which advice to revisit. Researchers who need the evidence behind every reading, and something to cite. And the reports' own authors, who can reply on every page.

How AI is used

The reviews are done by AI, in the same steps for every report, so the work can be repeated and compared. A named person checks each stage. Every page says what the AI did and what people checked.
