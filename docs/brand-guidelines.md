## 1. Positioning

### The problem

Governments publish long-range visions and scenario sets (España 2050, Indonesia Emas 2045, the ASEAN national foresight reports). They take years to make and shape budgets, laws and plans. Then they are rarely read again. Nobody checks, in public and in plain words, whether the assumptions still hold, whether the country is on the path the report set out, or whether the advice still fits a world that has moved.

Think-tank commentary does this occasionally, slowly and with a point of view. Government monitoring checks its own targets and rarely its assumptions. Fact-checkers issue verdicts, which is the wrong tool for a document about the future.

### Positioning statement

**For** citizens, policymakers and researchers who want to know whether the futures their governments planned for still hold,
**Norantz is** a public, AI-assisted second reading of national vision and scenario reports
**that shows**, report by report, which assumptions held, where things stand against the goals, what has changed since publication, and which recommendations deserve a second look.
**Unlike** commentary, scorecards or fact-checks, Norantz reads every report on its own terms, describes rather than grades, and shows its sources, its coverage and its method on every page.

### Brand idea: the second reader

Every report has a first reader: the minister, the committee, the press on launch day. Norantz is the second reader, who comes back years later with the report in one hand and the evidence in the other and asks the question in the name: *towards where?*

The second reader is a **critical friend**. A critic looks for what the authors got wrong. A friend explains what they were trying to do. A critical friend does both, in that order.

**Principle:** Read generously. Check rigorously.

### Promise and proof

| We promise | Because every page has |
| --- | --- |
| You will understand the report in one minute | A one-line finding, a 120-word summary and three findings above the waterline |
| You can trust what you read | An "as of" date and a source on every claim; the human checks named; the themes not reviewed shown |
| You will see the report fairly | Its intent stated first; its own goals as the yardstick; a right of reply for the authors |
| You can use it | What to watch next, with the level that would change a reading; citable versions; downloadable data |
| It will keep up | Repeatable reviews at scale, re-run on a stated schedule |

### What Norantz is not

- **Not a scorecard.** No overall score, no percentage, no grade. Counts by label only, always next to coverage.
- **Not a forecast.** It says what the evidence shows so far and what new conditions may bear on. It does not predict which future will arrive.
- **Not a verdict on people.** "Behind" describes a figure, not a government. "Not supported so far" describes evidence, not an author.
- **Not an opinion.** The yardstick is always the report's own aims. Norantz does not say whether the aims were the right ones.

### Audiences and their lines

| Reader | What they ask | The line for them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curious citizen (reference: a junior consultant) | What was this report and where do things stand? | "The futures your country planned, read again in plain words." |
| Policymaker or adviser | What is new since the report, and what should we revisit? | "Which assumptions are under pressure, and which advice still stands." |
| Policy researcher | What is the evidence and can I cite it? | "Every rating sourced, dated and versioned. Download the register." |
| Report authors and publishers | Will this be fair? | "We start from what you set out to achieve. You have a right of reply on every page." |

### Personality: the critical friend in six traits

| Trait | Means | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exact** | Figures, dates and sources, every time | Pedantic or jargon-heavy |
| **Fair** | The report's own yardstick; challenges and opportunities given equal weight | Neutral to the point of saying nothing |
| **Generous** | Authors' intent stated first; context of the moment they wrote in | Deferential or promotional |
| **Plain-spoken** | Short sentences, everyday words, the answer first | Casual, cute or flippant |
| **Curious** | Questions are welcome, including our own open ones | Rhetorical or leading |
| **Open** | Coverage, limits and method shown; corrections welcomed | Defensive |

### Tone sliders (1-5)

| Pole | Position | Pole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal | 3 | Casual |
| Critical | 3 | Supportive |
| Serious | 2 | Playful |
| Expert | 4 | Everyday |

Critical and supportive sit at the centre on purpose. When a finding pushes the tone one way, the next sentence pulls it back: a hard finding is followed by the authors' reasoning; a reassuring one by its limits.

### Taglines

| Line | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Towards where?** | The meaning of the name. Primary tagline, beside the mark. |
| **A second reading of national futures.** | Descriptor. Page titles, search snippets, social bios. |
| **Read generously. Check rigorously.** | The principle. About page, method page, talks. |
| **Are the futures we planned still pointing the right way?** | Campaign line, for launches and the home page hero. |

### Relationship to Plural Futures

Endorsed sub-brand. Plural Futures designs futures with organisations; Norantz reads published futures in public. The endorsement tells readers the method comes from a foresight practice, not a newsroom or a campaign.

- The lockup carries "by Plural Futures" in JetBrains Mono beneath or beside the wordmark, in `ink-2`.
- The footer and the method page name Plural Futures in full and link to it.
- Plural Futures materials refer to "Norantz, our public review of national futures".
- Norantz never uses the Plural Futures eye, dots or chevron glyphs as its own mark. It may use them inside content where Plural Futures uses them (the eye for "watch").

## 2. Name

**Norantz** is Basque for "towards where?" / "in what direction?": *nora* (where to) + directional suffix *-ntz* (towards). It is a question, not a noun. Basque has a noun for direction, *norabidea*; the brand deliberately does not use it.

- Say **no-RANTS**. Light stress; final *tz* is a sharp "ts".
- Write **Norantz**: capital N, rest lower case. Never NORANTZ in running text. Lower-case norantz only in the wordmark and URLs.
- First mention in any new context gives the meaning: "Norantz (Basque for 'towards where?')".
- No puns or possessive tricks. "Norantz's review" is fine.
- "Nora" is a common first name; never use it as a short form.

**Naming things under the brand:** plain descriptive names.

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Norantz review (the page for one report) | NorantzCheck, NorantzLens |
| The register | Norantz Index, Norabegira |
| The method | The Norantz Engine |
| What has changed (the change scan, on the page) | Horizon Radar |

**Name risk in Spain:** a camper-van maker in Gipuzkoa trades as Norantz (4×4 line "Ipar"; Camper of the Year, Barcelona 2019; Ipar 2 shown at Vanlife Expo Grenoble, May 2026). `norantz.org` is taken by an academic database on northern Basque grammar. Mitigations, in order: (1) never ship the bare word: titles and metadata read "Norantz · a second reading of national futures"; (2) win on report names ("España 2050 review", "Indonesia Emas 2045 progress") in URLs and titles; (3) lean on "Norantz, by Plural Futures" in Spanish launches; (4) trademark and domain check by a lawyer before launch (vehicles and research services usually sit in different classes; not legal advice); (5) keep a fallback: the mark, Sky and the voice do not depend on the word.

## 3. Logo

### The mark: the bearing

A rounded tile in Sky holding a point of origin, the heading the report set (dotted) and the heading things actually took (solid, with an arrow). The gap between the two lines is the question in the name. Drawn, not typeset, to Plural Futures glyph rules: 14px brand radius tile, single-ink strokes, no gradients. Below 24px the dotted heading closes up; at favicon size let the arrow carry it.

### The wordmark

Lower case, monoline, geometric, drawn on a 40-unit x-height with a 7-unit stroke (weight between Bricolage Grotesque Medium and Bold). The bottom bar of the *z* runs on past the letter in Sky: *-ntz*, "towards", as a line that keeps going. **It is a drawn draft** for a type designer to refine (spacing and the `t`).

### Rules

- Clear space on every side: mark tile height ÷ 4.
- Minimum size: wordmark 72px wide on screen, 20mm in print. Below that, mark alone.
- Run-on is always Sky (or `sky-ink` on paper) or the same ink as the letters. Never Ember, never the spectrum.
- Do not set "norantz" in a typeface in place of the drawn wordmark. In running text the name is a word in the text face.
- Do not stretch, outline, add shadows to or rotate the mark; do not put the wordmark on a photograph.

Live text, not part of the SVGs. Appears in the site header, footer, social cards and the first slide of a deck. Inside a report page it can be dropped (the footer carries it). Alternative wording "A Plural Futures project" is still open.

## 4. Colour

### The rule: colour tells time; shape tells status

On a Norantz page colour never means good or bad.

| Role | Tokens | Means | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Then** | `loam`, `loam-ink`, `loam-wash` | What the report said | The report's path, its quotes, "In the report" labels, its cover tile |
| **Now** | `sky`, `sky-ink`, `sky-wash` | What the evidence shows | Observed lines, latest figures, "Now" labels, links, focus |
| Structure | `ink`, `ink-2`, `ink-3`, `line`, `line-2` | Neither | Text, rules, axes, status markers |
| Paper | `paper`, `paper-2`, `paper-3`, `surface` | Neither | Grounds, alternating sections, lifted cards |
| Call to action | `ember`, `ember-ink` | Do this | One per screen at most. Never in data or status |

Belongs to plurality. Warm ink `#241A17` as ground. Sky stays `#42C0EB`; `sky-ink` lightens to `#7FD3F1`; Loam lightens to `#C9A79C`. For closers, covers, social cards and the data view at night.

## 6. Voice and writing

### The iceberg

| Layer | Holds | Length | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The tip** | One governing finding about the report today | One sentence, ≤25 words | Under the page title; search snippet; social card; first line of any briefing |
| **Above the waterline** | Summary and three findings, each dated | 120 words plus three lines | "At a glance" |
| **Just below** | One finding per section as its heading | Action titles, ≤15 words | Section headings, slide titles, chart titles |
| **The body** | Evidence: figures, dates, one line per source | Paragraphs of 1-3 sentences | Section bodies, cards, tables |
| **The deep** | Register, sources, versions, method, limits | As long as needed | Register, method page, downloads |

The **waterline** is visible on the page: a 1.5px `sky-ink` rule with the Mono label **BELOW: THE EVIDENCE**. Everything above it must stand alone when shared, screenshotted or quoted.

### Five iceberg rules

1. **Answer first.** The first sentence of every page, section, card and paragraph carries its point.
2. **Headings are findings.** "The growth assumption holds; the jobs assumption holds in part", not "Key assumptions".
3. **Figure, then label.** "Unemployment was 11.3% in 2025 against a path of 9.8%: behind", not "Behind: unemployment 11.3%".
4. **One idea per paragraph, one claim per sentence.** Average ≤20 words; no sentence over 35.
5. **Every layer is complete.** No "see below for why". A caveat a layer needs goes in that layer.

### The order of a finding (empathy by structure)

1. **Intent:** what the authors set out to achieve, in their terms.
2. **Then:** what the report assumed or committed to, with a page reference.
3. **Now:** what the evidence shows, dated and sourced.
4. **So what:** what it means for the report's own goals, and what to watch.

Approved illustrative example: "The plan aimed to make the country a high-income economy by 2045 *(intent)*. It assumed average growth of 6% a year from 2025 *(then)*. Growth averaged 5.0% in 2023–2025, according to the statistics office, as of October 2026 *(now)*. At that pace the income goal would arrive later than 2045; the report's own sensitivity table puts it near 2050. Watch the 2027 investment figures *(so what)*." (Figures are illustrative.)

### Evidence habits

- Date everything: "as of" with month and year on every figure.
- Source everything: one source per claim, linked, primary first.
- Show what was not checked: "Not yet reviewed" is a status.
- Separate looking back from looking ahead: "so far" for evidence, "from here" for pressure. Never one label.
- Say how sure: emerging issues marked less certain. *May*, *could*, *so far* only where the evidence is genuinely open.

### Describe, do not grade

| Write | Not |
| --- | --- |
| The figure is below the path | The government failed, missed, fell short |
| The evidence does not support this so far | The report was wrong, got it wrong |
| The report does not discuss X | The report missed, ignored, overlooked X |
| Holds / holds in part / goes further | Correct / partly correct / exceeded |
| Under pressure from | Threatened by, undermined by |
| Worth revisiting | Outdated, obsolete, discredited |
| Behind / on path / ahead (of the report's path) | Failing / on track / succeeding |
| The authors expected | The authors claimed, believed, assumed wrongly |
| Challenge, opportunity | Threat, win, risk, upside |

**Banned words.** Plural Futures list: *innovate, leverage, unlock, transform, ecosystem, disrupt, game-changer, robust*. Norantz adds: *shocking, damning, slammed, reality check, scorecard, verdict, report card, fact-check*. (Whether a report's own term, such as Indonesia's "game changers", may be used as a proper name is not settled; see `open-questions.md`.)

### Mechanics

- British spelling: *organisation, programme, labour*.
- Sentence case everywhere except Mono overline labels.
- Oxford comma.
- Numerals for all figures, dates, years and percentages, even under ten. Words for other numbers under ten in prose.
- Report titles in italics on first mention: *España 2050*. Scenario names in the report's own words, quoted if phrases.
- Name the authors and publishers. Use their own term for their document (vision, plan, strategy, scenario set).
- Questions are welcome as headings when the answer follows immediately.
- No emoji. No exclamation marks. Approved symbols: → ↗ • — × and smart quotes.

### Plain-language labels

| Method term | On the page |
| --- | --- |
| Normative report | A vision |
| Exploratory report | A scenario set |
| Hybrid report | A vision with comparison scenarios |
| Change scan | What has changed since publication |
| Signpost | Early sign |
| Driver | What separates the scenarios |
| Proxy indicator | A measure we chose (always labelled as such) |

### Surfaces

| Surface | Tip | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report page | One line under the title | The page template |
| Social card | The tip, report title, "as of" date | None; link to the page |
| Social post | The tip in your own sentence, plus one figure | Two sentences max. Never a rating word without its figure |
| Briefing note for a ministry | The tip as the subject line | Intent, then, now, so what; one page |
| Slide | The action title | One chart or three short lines |
| Newsletter | A question as headline, the tip as first line | Three findings from different reports |

### Layout

- 12-column grid, 24px gutter, 1200px container. Reading column 680px; data and the then/now pair may use the full container.
- **Then and now side by side**: "In the report" (Loam label) left, "Now" (Sky label) right on wide screens; stacked, then first, on phones.
- Asymmetry over mirroring: 7/5 or 8/4 splits, not 6/6, except the then/now pair and the challenge/opportunity pair, which must be equal.
- Whitespace is the design.

### Shape and surfaces

- Rules: 1px `line` between items; 1.5px `ink` between sections; 2.5px for the quote rule.
- Cards: 1px `line-2` outline on paper, or lifted to `surface` with no border. No drop shadows on cards.
- **Dotted means expected; solid means observed** for chart lines, marker strokes and chip outlines.

### Motifs

- **Bearing rays:** three to five lines from one origin, one dotted, at 0.16 opacity in ink, or full Sky on the cover. Heroes and section openers.
- **The waterline.**
- **Report tiles:** a report's cover, small, in a bordered 14px tile with a Loam label "THEN · 2017" above it. Credit the publisher; check their terms before showing a cover.
- No gradients except the spectrum; no blur; no noise textures; no glass.

### Imagery

Plural Futures rules: documentary, warm, never stock, never AI-slick, duotone in one ink when treated. Norantz adds: **no photographs of politicians or officials** (a face next to a finding reads as a verdict on that person); **data first** (where a picture and a chart compete, the chart wins).

### Motion

240-720ms, ease-out, no bounce. In a heading chart the Loam path draws first, then the Sky line over it: then before now, every time. Respect reduced motion: show the final state.

### Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA. Text 4.5:1 on its stated grounds in both themes. Colour never carries meaning alone: every marker has a text label, every chart line a direct label. Focus: 2px `sky-ink` ring with a 2px paper gap. Touch targets 44px. Body never below 16px on phones.

## 8. Status and data

### Three rules

1. **No traffic lights.** Markers in ink, Loam and Sky only.
2. **Shape carries status; a word always comes with it.**
3. **Looking back and looking ahead are different families.** Look-back = bearing markers (solid arrows). Look-ahead = field chips (dashed outline). Never merged into one label.

### Bearing markers: how an assumption has fared so far

Report's heading as a dotted Loam line from a common origin; evidence as a solid `sky-ink` arrow over it.

| Register term | Public label (proposed, not agreed) | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed | **Holds** | Arrow runs the full length of the heading |
| Strengthened | **Goes further** | Arrow runs past the end of the heading, double head |
| Weakened | **Holds in part** | Arrow on the heading, stopping halfway |
| Invalidated | **Not supported so far** | Arrow leaves at a wide angle from the heading |
| Not yet reviewed | **Not yet reviewed** | Dotted heading only, open origin, no arrow |

Sizes: 20px in tables and lists, 32px in cards, 48px in a section opener. Label to the right, JetBrains Mono 500, 12px, uppercase. (The Indonesia page draft 0.1 used "Not supported by evidence so far" once, the longer form from spec open question 1.)

### Target position: a figure against the report's path

Dotted Loam path rising left to right; Sky dot for the latest figure. Figure and gap come first in the text.

| Status | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| **Met** | Dot at the end of the path, inside a ring |
| **Ahead** | Dot above the path |
| **On path** | Dot on the path |
| **Behind** | Dot below the path |
| **No longer in force** | Path and dot in `ink-3`, strike through the path's end |

Where lower is better (emissions, unemployment), "ahead" still sits above the line; say so in the chart note, do not flip the marker.

### Field chips: what new conditions may mean from here

Dashed 1px outline ("not yet in the data").

| Pressure reading | Glyph |
| --- | --- |
| **Under pressure** | Two arrows pressing on a bar from one side |
| **Supported** | Two arrows running alongside the bar, in its direction |
| **Mixed** | One pressing, one alongside |
| **Watch** | An open eye (the Plural Futures eye, meaning scanning) |
| **Untouched** | The bar alone |

Each chip names its strongest change: "Under pressure · ageing workforce →", linked to "What has changed".

### Recommendation and commitment chips

7px radius, Mono label.

| Status | Outline | Glyph |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Still stands** | Solid `ink` | Filled square |
| **Revisit** | Solid `ink` | Open circle with arrow (↺) |
| **Revisit: new conditions** | Dashed `sky-ink` | ↺ with a Sky dot |
| **Not assessed** | Hairline `line-2`, text `ink-3` | None |

Commitment delivery (delivered, partly delivered, not delivered, dropped, not assessed) is Mono text only. No bars, no ticks, no crosses (they read as marking homework).

### Tallies

One row of markers with a number beside each, then the coverage line ("14 of 18 themes reviewed"). No percentages, no filling bar, no total score. "Not yet reviewed" always shown at the same size.

### Challenges and opportunities

Equal cards side by side; on phones two tabs of equal width. Same colour, size and type; they differ only in label and glyph (a challenge's arrow crosses the report's heading; an opportunity's runs alongside). Lead with the change: "Since 2022, …" before "The vision has no measure for this."

### Charts

**Heading chart (the signature):** one indicator, publication year to horizon year. Report's path: dotted Loam 2px, ending in an open ring at the horizon target. Evidence: solid `sky-ink` 2.5px, ending in a filled dot at the latest figure, directly labelled (figure, year, "as of"). NOW: hairline vertical rule labelled in Mono. Gap: bracket at the latest year, labelled in the indicator's units. No projection of the evidence line; the required rate goes in the sentence under the chart. Action title; source and "as of" in Mono beneath.

**Range chart (scenario sets):** scenario lines in their inks; band between highest and lowest in `spectrum-soft` at 0.6; evidence as solid `sky-ink` on top. Reading written under the chart: "inside the range", "at the edge", "outside it".

**General:** axes and gridlines in `line`; axis labels Mono 12px `ink-2`; direct labels, no legends where a label fits; bars start at zero, lines may zoom and say so; every chart has a source line and "as of"; colours Loam (then), Sky (now), scenario inks (scenarios), ink (everything else). Nothing else.

### Scenario cards

Equal width and height. 6px band at the top in the scenario's ink; name in Bricolage 600; two-sentence story in Newsreader; horizon picture as three Mono-labelled lines. For a vision, the preferred future carries the Mono label **THE REPORT'S GOAL** above the name; the card is not made bigger.
