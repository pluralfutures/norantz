## 1. At a glance

**Tip:** Indonesia's 2045 plan rests on 6–7% growth a year; two years in, growth runs at 5.1–5.5%, and two shocks it does not discuss have arrived.

(25 words.)

**Lead**

*Indonesia Emas 2045* is the long-term plan Indonesia wrote into law in September 2024. It aims for high income by 2038–2041 and an advanced economy by the centenary in 2045. It expects growth of 6–7% a year, led by productivity, to carry five headline targets. Two years in, growth is 5.1–5.5%, below the plan's floor of 5.6%. The tax ratio fell in 2025, while a free-meals programme of about 1% of GDP was added. US tariffs and an oil-price spike, which the plan does not discuss, have arrived. Manufacturing's share of GDP has risen since 2022, the direction the plan needs. Most 2045 targets are too early to judge; the measures that rest on public finances are worth revisiting.

(119 words.)

**Three things to know**

1. Growth was 5.11% in 2025 and about 5.5% in the first three quarters of 2026, against the plan's floor of 5.6%. A 6% average to 2045 now needs 6.1% a year, faster than any decade since 1990. *As of November 2026, BPS.* **[GAP]** Quarterly figures read from BPS release pages and press reports; "any decade since 1990" from the reviewer's record of the BPS series. Verify at source.
2. The tax ratio fell to 9.31% of GDP in 2025, against the plan's 10.7–11.2% for that year and 18–20% by 2045. A free-meals programme of about 1% of GDP was added in 2026. *As of January 2026, Kemenkeu.* **[GAP]** Ratio computed by DDTC/Kontan from Kemenkeu and BPS figures; verify at Kemenkeu.
3. Two shocks the plan does not discuss arrived in its first two years: a 19% US tariff on most Indonesian goods (agreed July 2025), and an oil-price spike in February–April 2026 that took Bank Indonesia's policy rate from 4.75% to 5.5% by June. *As of June 2026, Bank Indonesia; change scan X2 and X10.* **[GAP]** The size of BI's tightening differs between carriers (75 or 100 basis points); verify at BI.

## 2. How to read this page

**Overline:** 02 · How to read this page

**Action title:** A vision in force for two years: its goals are aims, not forecasts

This report is a vision. Its goals are the authors' aims for 2045, not forecasts. The plan is two years old, so most goals are too early to judge. This page tests what the plan assumed, and asks what has changed since it was written.

Where a figure is "behind", that describes the distance between the latest published number and the plan's own path for that year. It does not describe a government. Where a change is a "challenge" or an "opportunity", it is judged against the vision's own goals, not the reviewer's preferences.

Two readings are kept apart. A **rating** says what the evidence shows so far about a claim the plan makes. A **pressure reading** says which new conditions bear on that claim from here, whether or not the data has yet moved.

| Shape | Public label | Register term | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrow along the full heading | Holds | Confirmed | The evidence matches what the plan expected. |
| Arrow past the end, double head | Goes further | Strengthened | The variable has moved further in the direction the plan expected. |
| Arrow stopping halfway | Holds in part | Weakened | The direction holds; the size or timing does not. |
| Arrow leaving at a wide angle | Not supported so far | Invalidated | The evidence so far runs against what the plan expected. |
| Dotted heading, open origin | Not yet reviewed | Not yet reviewed | Too early to test, or not yet checked. |

| Shape | Target position | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dot above the path | Ahead | The latest figure is more than 3% better than the plan's path for its year. |
| Dot on the path | On path | Within 3% of the path. |
| Dot below the path | Behind | More than 3% worse than the path. |
| Dot inside a ring at the path's end | Met | The target has been reached. |
| Path and dot in grey, end struck through | No longer in force | A target of the superseded 2017 deck; last-known status shown. |

| Chip (dashed outline) | Pressure reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two arrows pressing on a bar | Under pressure | New conditions push against the claim. |
| Two arrows alongside the bar | Supported | New conditions run with it. |
| One pressing, one alongside | Mixed | Both at once. |
| An open eye | Watch | Something new may bear on it; not yet clear which way. |
| The bar alone | Untouched | Nothing found that bears on it. |

Ratings rest on official sources only: statistics offices, central banks, ministries, and international bodies. Think tanks give context. News reports never support a rating; where one carried an official figure, the page says "verify at source".

# Part B. The report (then)

## 3. Why it was written

**Overline:** 03 · Why it was written

**Action title:** Written into law in 2024 to replace the 2005–2025 plan, as growth slowed

- **Who asked for it:** The constitution and the planning law (UU 25/2004) require a long-term national plan every 20 years. The previous one, RPJPN 2005-2025, expired at the end of 2024. **[GAP]** Who chose the name "Indonesia Emas" (the centenary of independence), and the political sponsor, are not recorded.
- **Who wrote it:** Kementerian PPN/Bappenas, the national planning ministry, drafted the annex. Parliament and the President enacted it as UU 59/2024.
- **The moment:** Drafted in 2023 on data to 2022, after the COVID-19 recession of 2020 (−2.1%) and during the 2022 commodity windfall. The plan opens with a diagnosis: growth slowing by period (5.7% → 4.7% → 5.0%), falling productivity, early deindustrialisation, and too much regulation (UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 14–26). Enacted in the last months of the Jokowi government; implemented from October 2024 by the Prabowo government.
- **How it was made:** Led by Bappenas, with a national consultation (Musrenbangnas keynote, Bali, 22 May 2023) and the usual planning cascade into five-year and annual plans.
- **Who was consulted:** **[GAP]**

**The 2017 deck:** a scientific oration by the Minister of National Development Planning at Universitas Indonesia on 26 September 2017, building on President Jokowi's "Impian Indonesia 2015-2085".

**[STRAIN]** "The moment" is assembled from the standing check and the register; the review has no account of the parliamentary passage. **[GAP]** Consultation detail and the modelling behind the growth paths are not recorded.

## 4. The questions it explored

**Overline:** 04 · The questions it explored

**Action title:** Four of its eight questions are still central; three are not yet reviewed

The plan is organised as eight missions answering a diagnosis of key issues. The eight questions below are the review's reading of that diagnosis.

| Question the report set out to inform | Status today | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. How can growth rise from about 5% to 6–7% and stay there for 20 years? | Still central | Growth was 5.11% in 2025 and about 5.5% in 2026 to Q3. Official readings put potential growth near 5% (§7.9). |
| 2. Can productivity become the engine, in place of more labour and capital? | Still central | Productivity growth was negative over 2010–2023 and positive in 2021–2023 (§7.2). |
| 3. Can manufacturing's share of GDP be turned around? | Changed shape | The share has risen since 2022, from 18.3% to 19.07%. The question is now pace, and markets for the lead products (§9, §10). |
| 4. How is the demographic window used before it closes? | Not yet reviewed | Theme not evidenced. The 2025 intercensal survey puts the dependency ratio one point above projection (X8). |
| 5. Can the state pay for the plan? | Still central | Tax ratio 9.31% in 2025; deficit 2.92% of GDP; private finance about 28% of infrastructure need (§7.3, §7.11). |
| 6. Can regulation and institutions deliver? | Still central | The stock of regulation has not visibly fallen; the corruption index was 34 in 2025, against 38 projected (§7.6). |
| 7. Can emissions fall on the net-zero-2060 path while growth speeds up? | Not yet reviewed | Theme not evidenced. The second NDC (October 2025) sets a 2030 level above the plan's path (X9). |
| 8. Can regional gaps narrow, with eastern Indonesia's share rising? | Not yet reviewed | Theme not evidenced. The RPJMN's 2029 figure for eastern Indonesia is 22.4%, against 23.3% on the vision's path. |

**Questions that have emerged since:** How does the plan absorb shocks it does not model, such as tariffs and energy prices? What do large new programmes (free meals, village cooperatives) and a new state investment holding (Danantara) mean for its finance? Does generative AI change the plan's "40% of jobs" view of technology?

## 5. The futures it set out

**Overline:** 05 · The futures it set out

**Action title:** One goal at two speeds: high income by 2038 or by 2041

The plan sets one destination and two paths to it. A slower path, near or below 5% growth, is drawn as the warning. The 2017 deck set the same destination eight years earlier, with faster numbers.

**Card 1.** Label: THE REPORT'S GOAL

- **Name:** Indonesia Emas 2045
- **The story:** Indonesia leaves the middle-income trap in 2038–2041 and reaches 2045 as a united, sovereign, advanced, and sustainable state, with the world's fifth-largest economy. Growth of 6–7% a year, led by productivity, pays for change across eight missions (UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 65–72, 90–92).
- **In 2045:** Income per head USD 23,000–30,300 · manufacturing 28% of GDP · poverty 0.5–0.8% · emissions intensity down 93.5% · eastern Indonesia 28.5% of GDP (printed p. 66).
- **What it needs:** 6–7% growth on average; investment near 30% of GDP, mostly from outside the budget; a tax ratio rising to 18–20%; 20 priority measures delivered; a world that stays open to trade; continuity across four governments.

**Card 2.** Label: WITHOUT ACTION

- **Name:** Baseline and moderate paths
- **The story:** Growth stays near or below 5%. Indonesia leaves the middle-income trap only at the end of the period, or not before 2045 (Grafik 4.2.1, printed p. 90).
- **In 2045:** **[GAP]** Not quantified in the law beyond the growth paths.
- **What it assumes:** Current trends without the plan's reforms.

**Card 3.** Label: THEN · 2017 · NO LONGER IN FORCE

- **Name:** Visi Indonesia 2045 (2017)
- **The story:** The same destination with faster numbers: high income by 2035 and the fourth-largest economy by 2045 (slides 9–10, 26).
- **In 2045:** GDP per head USD 28,934 · industry 32% of GDP · investment 39% of GDP · growth averaging 6.4% (6.5% in 2020–2025, 7% in 2026–2035).
- **What happened to it:** COVID-19 broke its path in the first five years. The 2024 law kept the destination and moved it later (§14).

**How the futures relate:** The 6% path (high income by 2041) and the 7% path (by 2038) are one end-state at two speeds, not two futures. The RPJMN 2025-2029 added a steeper path, rising from 5.3% to 8%, that the law does not contain.

All three cards carry a Loam band, as on the España page; the 2017 card's band is in grey (`ink-3`), the colour brand §8 gives "no longer in force". **[STRAIN]** The "without action" card has no 2045 picture, so it is thinner than the goal card; brand §8 asks for equal cards.

### 7.1 Growth has run at 5.0–5.5%, below the 6–7% every 2045 target is scaled to

- **In the report (2024):** Growth of 6–7% a year to 2045; 5.6–6.1% in 2025–2029. Stated. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 71–72, 90–92. (F1, 3.1)
- **How much rests on it:** Every 2045 target.
- **Now:** 5.05% in 2023, 5.03% in 2024, 5.11% in 2025; 5.61%, 5.29%, and 5.47% in the first three quarters of 2026. The RPJMN path is 5.3% (2025) rising to 8% (2029); the 2027 annual plan lowered 2027 from 7.5% to 6%. Institutions project 5.0–5.7% for 2026–2028. *BPS, as of 5 November 2026; Bappenas, August 2026.* **[GAP]** 2026 quarterly figures read from BPS release pages and press reports; verify at BPS.
- **Rating so far:** Holds in part (register: weakened). Not "not supported": under two years of a 21-year average.
- **From here:** Under pressure · X7 outturn; X1 path lowered year by year; X10 2026 shock cut 2026 forecasts to 4.7–5.0%.
- **So what:** Every year below 6% raises the pace the remaining years must carry. A full year at 5.6% or more in 2026 would bring the claim back to its floor.

### 7.2 Productivity growth was negative over 2010–2023, as the plan said, and positive since 2021

- **In the report (2024):** Low productivity is the binding constraint, and productivity growth is the lever. Stated. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed p. 15. (F3)
- **How much rests on it:** The growth story.
- **Now:** Total factor productivity fell 0.3% a year over 2010–2023, then grew 5.1%, 1.2%, and 0.3% in 2021–2023; projected 1.7% a year for 2025–2030. Bappenas's own productivity plan puts manufacturing at −1.22% a year for 2013–2022. *APO Productivity Databook, as of 8 October 2025; Bappenas, October 2025.*
- **Rating so far:** Holds, as a description of 2010–2023 (register: confirmed). That productivity is *the* lever is causal and not yet tested.
- **From here:** Watch · X3 generative AI, a productivity tool the plan does not name.
- **So what:** The diagnosis stands. Whether the remedy works will show only in the next productivity editions.

### 7.3 The tax ratio fell in 2025 while a new free-meals programme was added

- **In the report (2024):** Fiscal space is enough to fund the plan: a tax ratio rising to 18–20%, and the fiscal rules kept. Measures stated; sufficiency implied. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 117–119, 122, 327. (F9)
- **How much rests on it:** Every budget-funded measure.
- **Now:** Tax ratio 9.31% of GDP in 2025 (10.08% in 2024), against 10.7–11.2% projected. Deficit 2.92% of GDP against a 3% cap; debt 40.46% against a 60% ceiling; rules unchanged. The free-meals programme (MBG) spent Rp 51.5 trillion in 2025 and has Rp 268–335 trillion for 2026, about 1.1–1.4% of GDP. *Kemenkeu, APBN KiTa, as of January and October 2026; IMF, January 2026.* **[GAP]** Tax ratio computed by DDTC/Kontan, debt via Kontan, MBG via Katadata; verify at Kemenkeu.
- **Rating so far:** Holds in part (register: weakened). The rules and the debt limit hold; revenue moved the other way, and a large recurrent cost the plan does not contain was added.
- **From here:** Under pressure · X5 new programmes and state vehicles; X10 fuel subsidy set on USD 70 oil.
- **So what:** Most of the 20 priority measures draw on this budget. The 2026 tax ratio will show whether 2025 was a dip or a turn.

### 7.4 World trade grew faster than the plan expected, but on negotiated rather than open terms

- **In the report (2024):** World trade grows about 3.4% a year, and markets stay open. Figure stated; openness implied. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 31, 39, 98. (F4)
- **How much rests on it:** Industrialisation, and the 2035–2039 "global expansion" phase.
- **Now:** World merchandise trade grew 2.8% in 2024 and 4.6% in 2025; forecast 3.9% in 2026 and 4.1% in 2027, with 42–47% of the growth from AI-related goods. A 19% US tariff on Indonesian goods was agreed in July 2025 and fixed by treaty in February 2026, with 0% on 1,819 lines; Indonesia removes tariffs on over 99% of US goods. The EU carbon border charge applies from January 2026. The EU-Indonesia trade agreement was concluded in September 2025. *WTO, as of 8 October 2026; European Commission, 29 June 2026.* **[GAP]** Treaty terms read via KPMG; verify at the US Trade Representative and the Indonesian trade ministry.
- **Rating so far:** Mixed. The stated half holds: trade grows above 3.4%. The implied half holds in part: rules-based access has given way to negotiated terms.
- **From here:** Mixed · X2 tariffs and duties, against X11 AI-related trade.
- **So what:** The volume the plan needs is there. The terms on which Indonesia reaches it have changed.

### 7.5 Nickel exports reached a record, with 92.6% going to one market

- **In the report (2024):** Access to markets, finance, and technology from all sides. Implied. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 31–33, 95–96. (F12)
- **How much rests on it:** Downstream processing, the plan's lead industrial measure.
- **Now:** Nickel-product exports set a record in 2025 and rose 50% in January–August 2026; 92.6% went to China. The 2026 ore quota was cut from 379 million tonnes to 290–300 million as prices fell. Western carbon, safeguard, and anti-dumping duties apply to stainless steel. Sovereign borrowing is not constrained. Access to technology was not evidenced. *BPS trade data via trade press, as of November 2026; IMF, January 2026.* **[GAP]** BPS figures read via Databoks and S&P Global; verify at BPS.
- **Rating so far:** Holds in part (register: weakened). Access is there, but in one market, and the state is managing supply to hold prices.
- **From here:** Under pressure · X2 Western duties on metals; X6 one-market dependence.
- **So what:** Downstream processing has grown, on terms the plan's "all sides" did not describe.

### 7.6 Regulation has not been simplified, and the corruption index fell to 34

- **In the report (2024):** The state can simplify regulation and raise its capacity to deliver. Problem stated; solvable implied. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 23–24, 77, 111–114. (F13, 1.7)
- **How much rests on it:** All 20 priority measures.
- **Now:** 23,806 ministerial and 264,878 regional regulations, on a wider count than the law's 18,045 and 18,661; no single regulatory body found. Corruption Perceptions Index 34 in 2025 (rank 109), down from 37, against 38 projected for 2025 and 60 targeted for 2045. *BPK, as of 4 August 2026; Transparency International, 10 February 2026.* **[GAP]** CPI read via Antara; verify at Transparency International. The BPK count is on a different basis from the law's.
- **Rating so far:** Holds in part (register: weakened, F13). The condition the plan describes persists (register: confirmed, 1.7).
- **From here:** Under pressure · X12 corruption index; X5 capacity spent on programmes outside the plan.
- **So what:** The plan's own governance indicator has moved away from its 2025 value.

### 7.7 The plan's cascade is working: five-year and annual plans arrived on schedule

- **In the report (2024):** The vision stays in force across governments, through the five-year and annual plans. Mechanism stated; that it holds implied. UU No. 59 Tahun 2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 319–326. (F10)
- **How much rests on it:** Every phase after 2029.
- **Now:** The RPJMN 2025-2029 (February 2025), RKP 2026, and RKP 2027 were issued on schedule and quote the vision's five targets. Provinces were asked to align their 2027 plans. Inside the cascade, the 2027 growth target fell from 7.5% to 6%, while 8% for 2029 was kept. *Bappenas, as of May–August 2026.*
- **Rating so far:** Holds (register: confirmed).
- **From here:** Supported · X1 the cascade is operating. It is also the channel through which the growth path is being lowered.
- **So what:** Continuity holds so far. The next test is the 2028 annual plan.

### 7.8 Investment grew more slowly than planned and reached 28.77% of GDP, not 30%

- **From here:** Watch · X5 Danantara as a new, unaudited investment channel.
- **So what:** Each unit of growth is costing more investment than the plan assumed.

## 14. What happened to the report

**Overline:** 14 · What happened to the report

**Action title:** In force since January 2025; the first five-year plan set a steeper growth path

- **Status today:** In force as law. UU 59/2024 replaced the RPJPN 2005-2025 (UU 17/2007) from 1 January 2025.

## Notes on the spec from this fill

Points 1 to 7 are carried from draft 0.1. Points 8 to 12 are new, from the rebuild. Each is a proposal.

1. **Pairing a predecessor** (decision I2) has no home in spec v0.2. It needed a header tile, a card in §5, blocks in §8, §9 and §14, and a tally line. Proposal: a predecessor/successor slot in the header and a standard "no longer in force" block in §5 and §9.
2. **Section 10 is the longest section** even at twelve changes with one-line evidence. Three changes appear in both lists. The two-tab layout (spec open question 9) is built in the HTML; a single list with a direction column is the alternative to test.
3. **Section 12 loses its power to tell measures apart** when one assumption (F9) underlies most of them: 16 of 20 carry the same label for the same reason. A per-measure delivery check is needed first.
4. **Section 6 repeats section 7** (spec open question 7). Here, as on the España page, it is the one-screen phone view; proposal: keep §6 and collapse §7 by default on phones.
5. **"Not supported so far"** is used once (O-2), for a superseded historical target. Whether it reads as neutral for an in-force claim is untested.
6. **Tallies** (spec open question 3): "4 behind, 1 ahead, 82 not yet reviewed" is hard to misquote as a score. "10 of 16 hold in part" could still be quoted; the coverage line beside it does the protective work.
7. **The early-horizon variant** makes §9 and §12 thin by design. §10 carries the page. Whether a general reader finds a page centred on "what has changed" readable needs a reader test.
8. **Two families side by side.** This is the first page with a bearing marker and a field chip on every assumption. The pair reads clearly in §7, where each has its own row. In §6's chain the two together make each row wide; on a phone they stack.
9. **"Mixed" has two meanings.** It is a rating (F4: half holds, half holds in part) and a pressure reading (X2 against X11). Neither has a bearing marker. The rating needs either a marker or method fix 1 (two questions per item).
10. **The banned word is the plan's own name.** "Game changers" is on the brand's banned list but is the plan's title for its 20 measures. "Priority measures", with the plan's term given once in quotes, reads naturally. Decide whether a report's own term may stand as a proper name.
11. **"No longer in force" has a marker but no data shape.** The brand defines the target marker (grey path, struck end). It works in the §9 table; on a heading chart a superseded path would need a third line style.
12. **The tip carries two numbers and a change-scan finding.** At 25 words it is at the limit. A shorter tip ("Two years in, Indonesia is growing below the plan's floor, and two shocks it does not discuss have arrived") drops the 6–7% intent, which brand §6 puts first.
