## 1. At a glance

**In brief**

Indonesia Emas 2045 is the long-term plan Indonesia wrote into law in September 2024. It aims at high income by 2038-41 and five headline targets for 2045 — income per head of USD 23,000-30,300, manufacturing at 28 % of GDP, poverty under 1 %, a top-15 global-power ranking and a 93.5 % cut in emissions intensity — on growth of 6-7 % a year. Two years in, growth is running at 5.1-5.5 %, below both the vision's own floor and the steeper path the first five-year plan set; the tax base shrank in 2025 while a new meals programme of about 1 % of GDP was added; and two external shocks the plan does not provide for — US tariffs in 2025 and the 2026 oil-price spike — have already arrived. Manufacturing's share of GDP is rising for the first time since 2005.

(118 words)

**Three things to know**

1. Growth was 5.11 % in 2025 and about 5.5 % so far in 2026, against a vision floor of 5.6 % and a five-year-plan path of 5.3 % rising to 8 %. Reaching the vision's 6 % average now needs 6.1 % a year for nineteen years — faster than any decade since 1990 (BPS, 5 November 2026).
2. The tax ratio fell to 9.31 % of GDP in 2025, against the plan's 10.7-11.2 % projection and an 18-20 % target for 2045, and the deficit used 97 % of its legal ceiling while a free-meals programme of about 1 % of GDP was added (Kemenkeu, January and October 2026).
3. The plan projects a straight line from 2025. Two dated shocks have landed in its first two years: a 19 % US tariff on most Indonesian goods (agreed July 2025) and an oil-price spike after the temporary closure of the Strait of Hormuz (February-April 2026), which took Bank Indonesia's policy rate from 4.75 % to 5.5 % by June (change scan, X2 and X10).

## 2. How to read this page

This report is a vision. Its goals are the authors' aims for 2045, not forecasts. Where a figure is "behind", that describes the distance between the latest published number and the plan's own path for that year; it is not a judgement of a government. Where a change in the world is listed as a "challenge" or an "opportunity", it is judged against the vision's own goals, not against what the reviewer would prefer.

Two readings are kept apart throughout. A **rating** says what the evidence shows so far about a claim the plan makes. A **pressure reading** says which new conditions bear on that claim from here, whether or not the data has yet moved.

| Label | For claims | For goals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed / Met | Evidence matches the plan. | Target reached. |
| Strengthened / Ahead | Evidence goes further in the same direction. | Latest figure better than the plan's path for that year by more than 3 %. |
| Weakened / Behind | Direction holds but size or timing does not. | Latest figure worse than the path by more than 3 %. |
| Not supported by evidence so far (register: Invalidated) | Evidence contradicts the plan. | — |
| On path | — | Within 3 % of the path. |
| Not yet reviewed | Too early, or not yet checked. | Same. |
| No longer in force | — | Target of a superseded document; last-known status shown. |

Pressure readings: **under pressure**, **supported**, **mixed**, **watch**, **untouched**.

What this report allows us to test now: the claims it makes about Indonesia and the world (growth, productivity, trade, demography, fiscal space, delivery), and whether the world it was written for is the world it now faces. What it does not yet allow: judging 2045 targets two years in. The required-rate figures in section 9 say what pace the targets now need, not whether they will be reached.

## 3. Why it was written

- **Who asked for it:** The 1945 Constitution and the planning law (UU 25/2004) require a national long-term plan each twenty years; RPJPN 2005-2025 expired at the end of 2024. **[GAP]** The decision to brand it "Indonesia Emas" (centenary of independence) and the political sponsor are not recorded in the review.
- **Who wrote it:** Kementerian PPN/Bappenas drafted the annex; it was enacted by the DPR with the President as UU 59/2024. The 2017 deck was a scientific oration by the Minister of National Development Planning at Universitas Indonesia on 26 September 2017, building on President Jokowi's "Impian Indonesia 2015-2085".
- **The moment:** Data to 2022, drafted in 2023 after the COVID-19 recession of 2020 (−2.1 %) and during the commodity windfall of 2022; the plan's opening chapter is a diagnosis of slowing growth (5.7 % → 4.7 % → 5.0 % by period), negative TFP growth, premature deindustrialisation and hyper-regulation (UU 59/2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 14-26). Enacted in the last months of the Jokowi government; implemented from October 2024 by the Prabowo government. **[STRAIN]** Assembled from the standing check and the register; the review has no account of the parliamentary passage.
- **How it was made:** Bappenas-led, with the Musrenbangnas national consultation (keynote, Bali, 22 May 2023) and the conventional planning cascade. **[GAP]** Consultation detail and the modelling behind the growth scenarios are not recorded.
- **Who was consulted:** **[GAP]**

## 5. The futures it set out

**The desired future — "Indonesia Emas 2045"** (the report's goal)

- **The story:** Indonesia leaves the middle-income trap in 2038-41 and reaches 2045 as a united, sovereign, advanced and sustainable state ("NKRI yang Bersatu, Berdaulat, Maju, dan Berkelanjutan"), with the world's fifth-largest economy. Growth of 6-7 % a year, driven by productivity rather than labour and capital, finances a transformation across eight missions (UU 59/2024, Lampiran, printed pp. 65-72, 90-92).
- **In 2045:** GNI per capita USD 23,000-30,300; manufacturing 28 % of GDP; poverty 0.5-0.8 %; Gini 0.29-0.32; eastern Indonesia 28.5 % of GDP; top-15 Global Power Index; Human Capital Index 0.73; GHG intensity down 93.5 %; IKLH 83 (Gambar 3.4.1, p. 66).
- **What it needs to happen:** 6-7 % growth on average with TFP as the lever; investment of about 30 % of GDP financed largely outside the budget; a tax ratio rising to 18-20 %; 20 "game changers" delivered; a world that stays open to trade; continuity across four governments.

**The "without action" case** (the report's own)

- **The story:** Growth stays near or below 5 % ("Baseline" and "Moderat" paths, Grafik 4.2.1, p. 90), and Indonesia does not leave the middle-income trap before 2045 or does so only at the end.
- **In 2045:** Not quantified in the law beyond the growth paths. **[GAP]**

**How the futures relate:** The two paths to the goal (6 % average → exit 2041; 7 % → exit 2038) are the same end-state at two speeds, not alternative futures. The RPJMN 2025-2029 added a third, steeper path (5.3 → 8.0 %) that the law does not contain.

**Predecessor — Visi Indonesia 2045 (2017), no longer in force**

- **The story:** The same destination set out eight years earlier with faster numbers: high income by 2035, growth averaging 6.4 % (6.5 % in 2020-25, 7 % in 2026-35), GDP per capita USD 28,934, fourth-largest economy, industry 32 % of GDP, investment 39 % of GDP (slides 9-10, 26).
- **What happened to it:** COVID-19 broke its path in the first five years; the law of 2024 kept the destination and moved it later (section 14).

## 7. Key assumptions

Ranked by how many 2045 goals rest on each. Rating = evidence so far (Tier-1 sources only). Pressure = change-scan reading, naming the strongest change in section 10. Full register: `claude/indonesia-2045-register.md`.

**1. Growth of 6-7 % a year is attainable (F1, 3.1)** — Stated (pp. 71-72, 90-92). Every 2045 target is scaled to it.
- **Evidence now:** 5.05 % (2023), 5.03 % (2024), 5.11 % (2025); 5.61 / 5.29 / 5.47 % in Q1-Q3 2026. Vision floor for 2025-29: 5.6 %. Operative RPJMN path: 5.3 % (2025), 6.3 % (2026), 7.5, 7.7, 8.0 %; the RKP 2027 lowered 2027 to 6 %. Institutions project 5.0-5.7 % for 2026-28. BPS, 5 Nov 2026; Bappenas, Aug 2026.
- **So far:** Weakened. Not "invalidated": under two years of a 21-year average.
- **Pressure:** Under pressure — X7 (outturn), X1 (path lowered through the cascade), X10 (2026 shock cut 2026 forecasts to 4.7-5.0 %).

**2. Low productivity is the binding constraint and TFP is the lever (F3)** — Stated (p. 15). Underpins the growth story.
- **Evidence now:** APO Productivity Databook 2025: TFP growth −0.3 % a year over 2010-23; +5.1, +1.2, +0.3 % in 2021-23; projected +1.7 % for 2025-30. Bappenas's own productivity master plan (Oct 2025) puts manufacturing TFP at −1.22 % a year for 2013-22. APO, 8 Oct 2025.
- **So far:** Confirmed as a description of 2010-23; the decline has not continued since 2021 (watch). The claim that TFP is *the* lever is causal and untested.
- **Pressure:** Watch — X3 (AI as a productivity instrument the plan did not name).

**3. Fiscal space is sufficient (F9)** — Stated measures, implied sufficiency (pp. 117-119, 122, 327). Funds every commitment.
- **Evidence now:** Tax ratio 9.31 % of GDP in 2025 (10.08 % in 2024) vs 10.7-11.2 % projected; deficit 2.92 % of GDP against a 3 % cap; debt 40.46 % against a 60 % ceiling; rules unchanged. Free-meals programme (MBG): Rp 51.5 tn in 2025, Rp 268-335 tn allocated for 2026 (≈ 1.1-1.4 % of GDP). Kemenkeu via APBN KiTa, Jan and Oct 2026; IMF, Jan 2026.
- **So far:** Weakened. Rules and debt intact; the revenue line moved the other way and a recurrent line the plan does not contain was added.
- **Pressure:** Under pressure — X5 (MBG, village cooperatives' Rp 240 tn guaranteed loans, Danantara), X10 (fuel subsidy premised on USD 70 oil).

**4. The world stays open and trade grows ≈ 3.4 % a year (F4)** — Stated figure, implied openness (pp. 31, 39, 98). Carries industrialisation and the 2035-39 "global expansion" phase.
- **Evidence now:** WTO merchandise trade volume +2.8 % (2024), +4.6 % (2025), forecast +3.9 % (2026), +4.1 % (2027); 42-47 % of that growth from AI-enabling goods. US "reciprocal" tariff: 32 % threatened (Apr 2025), 19 % agreed (Jul 2025), fixed by the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (19 Feb 2026) with 0 % on 1,819 lines; Indonesia removes tariffs on >99 % of US goods. EU CBAM definitive from 1 Jan 2026 (covers stainless); US/EU/UK 50 % steel measures. EU-Indonesia CEPA concluded Sept 2025, entry into force targeted Jan 2027. WTO, 8 Oct 2026; EU Commission, 29 Jun 2026.
- **So far:** Mixed — stated half confirmed (trade growing above 3.4 %), implied half weakened (rules-based access replaced by negotiated terms and product barriers on the roadmap's lead products).
- **Pressure:** Mixed — X2 (challenge, established) against X11 (opportunity, established).

**5. Access to markets, finance and technology from all sides (F12)** — Implied (pp. 31-33, 95-96). Carries downstreaming (GC-7).
- **Evidence now:** Nickel-product exports at a record in 2025 and +50 % Jan-Aug 2026; 92.6 % to China; 2026 ore quota cut from 379 Mt to 290-300 Mt as prices fell; Western carbon, safeguard and anti-dumping duties on stainless. Sovereign finance unconstrained (IMF, Jan 2026). Technology access not evidenced. BPS trade data via trade press, Nov 2026.
- **So far:** Weakened in one direction: access is intact but concentrated in one market, and the state is managing supply to hold prices.
- **Pressure:** Under pressure — X2, X6.

**6. The state can deliver: regulation simplified, capacity raised (F13, 1.7)** — Stated problem, implied as solvable (pp. 23-24, 111-114, 77). Carries all 20 game changers.
- **Evidence now:** Regulatory stock (BPK database, Aug 2026): 23,806 ministerial and 264,878 regional instruments on a wider count than the law's 18,045 / 18,661; no single regulatory-management body found. CPI 2025: 34, rank 109, down from 37 (projection for 2025: 38; target 2045: 60). TI, 10 Feb 2026.
- **So far:** 1.7 confirmed (the condition persists); F13 weakened on the plan's own indicator.
- **Pressure:** Under pressure — X12 (CPI), X5 (delivery capacity spent on programmes outside the plan).

**7. The vision stays in force across governments (F10)** — Stated mechanism, implied that it holds (pp. 319-326).
- **Evidence now:** RPJMN 2025-2029 (Feb 2025), RKP 2026 and RKP 2027 issued on schedule, quoting the vision's five targets; provinces pressed to align RKPD 2027. Inside the cascade the 2027 growth target fell from 7.5 % (RPJMN) to 6 % while the 8 % figure for 2029 is kept. Bappenas, May-Aug 2026.
- **So far:** Confirmed.
- **Pressure:** Supported — X1 (the cascade is operating; it is also the channel through which the operative growth path is being lowered).

**8. Investment grows 6.4-6.7 % a year in phase 1 at ≈ 30 % of GDP (3.4)** — Stated (pp. 94-96, 109).
- **Evidence now:** GFCF growth 3.8 % (2023), 4.6 % (2024), 5.09 % (2025), 5.96 % (Q1 2026); GFCF 28.77 % of GDP in 2025 vs 30.1-30.2 % projected; implied ICOR ≈ 5.6 vs the 4.6 the 2023 keynote aimed at. BPS, Feb and May 2026.
- **So far:** Weakened; rising since Q4 2025 (watch).
- **Pressure:** Watch — X5 (Danantara as a new channel, unaudited).

**9. Potential growth fell below 5 % after COVID-19 (1.2)** — Stated (p. 15); the chart's ~3.3 % is not rated.
- **Evidence now:** No Tier-1 body publishes a number. IMF output gap −0.1 (2025-26) with growth 5.0-5.1 % and medium-term growth ≈ 5.2 % imply potential of about 5.0-5.2 %; BI says the cycle is "still below potential"; APO projects 5.1 % for 2025-30. IMF, 21 Jan 2026.
- **So far:** Weakened, on inferred evidence.
- **Pressure:** Untouched.

**10. Growth has slowed by period (1.1)** and **premature deindustrialisation (1.3)** — Stated (pp. 15, 17).
- **Evidence now:** 2023-25 growth inside the plan's 4.7-5.3 % band; 2026 running above it. Manufacturing share 18.3 % (2022) → 19.07 % (2025), manufacturing outgrowing GDP for two years; a 2010 classification break means the 2005-22 fall is not one series (N3-10). BPS, Feb and Nov 2026.
- **So far:** 1.1 confirmed (watch 2026); 1.3 confirmed as history, weakened as a current trend.
- **Pressure:** Untouched / supported.

**11. Non-budget financing fills a large part of the investment need (6.1)** — Stated (p. 327); the RPJMN assigns 86.65 % of the 2025-29 need to private and household sources.
- **Evidence now:** Private participation in infrastructure ≈ 28 % of the 2025-29 need (Kemenko Infrastruktur, Sept 2025); PT PII guarantees Rp 112 tn; Danantara (from Feb 2025): Rp 120 tn state capital, SOE dividends Rp 142.3 tn in 2025, consolidated accounts pending.
- **So far:** Weakened, watch.
- **Pressure:** Mixed — X5 (Danantara, both directions; emerging).

**12. Developing economies reach 71 % of global output (F5)** — Stated (p. 31). Low rank.
- **Evidence now:** IMF WEO Oct 2026: EMDE growth 3.7-4.4 % vs 1.8-2.1 % for advanced economies; PPP share ≈ 60 % in 2025 (via commentary; verify).
- **So far:** Confirmed in direction. **Pressure:** Untouched.

**Not yet reviewed, with pressure readings only:** F2 demographic window — under pressure (X8: SUPAS 2025 dependency ratio 45.05 vs 44.02 projected); F6b consuming class — under pressure (X4: BPS middle class down 1.2 m in 2025, 7.13 m since 2020); F7 technology replaces 40 % of jobs — mixed (X3: ILO puts 3-4 % of Indonesian jobs in the highest GenAI-exposure category, 21.7 % exposed, employment in exposed occupations still growing); F8 climate and GHG path — under pressure (X9: second NDC's 2030 level 1.35-1.49 Gt vs the plan's 1.03 Gt; basis to be checked); F11 no large shock — under pressure (X2, X10); F6a global middle class — untouched.

## 14. What happened to the report

- **Status today:** In force as law. UU 59/2024 replaced RPJPN 2005-2025 (UU 17/2007) from 1 January 2025.
- **What it led to:** Perpres 12/2025 (RPJMN 2025-2029, 10 Feb 2025) adopts its structure, five targets and 2025 baselines, and sets a steeper growth path (8 % by 2029), a flatter energy path (renewables 23 % by 2029 vs 70 % by 2045) and several revised baselines; it adds programmes the law does not contain (free meals, free health checks, food estates, 3-million houses, Danantara as a proposed law, a state revenue agency). RKP 2026 and RKP 2027 followed; the RKP 2027 lowered the 2027 growth target to 6 %. Provincial plans are being aligned to it.
- **Versions:** A draft annex (Rancangan Akhir, 2023-24) preceded the law; the enacted annex differs in four places (vision wording adds "Bersatu"; an IKLH target; a formal-jobs indicator; four environmental sub-indices) and carries one internal inconsistency (annual GHG baseline 36.65 % in the table vs 32.65 % in the chart).
- **The 2017 predecessor:** Visi Indonesia 2045 (oration deck, 26 Sept 2017) set high income by 2035, 6.5 % growth for 2020-25, industry 32 % and investment 39 % of GDP. The 2023 draft and the 2024 law kept the destination and moved it: high income 2038/41; GDP rank #4 → #5; industry 28 %; investment share down and a TFP story in its place; the dependency-ratio trough from 2028-31 to "already passed". COVID-19 (2020) broke the 2017 path in its first five years. **[TRIAL]** The spec moves this section up to after section 2 for a superseded vision; here it stays in place because the main report is in force and the predecessor is paired.

## 15. How this review was done

- **Track:** Normative, early-horizon variant (method proposal 21): claims tested, change scan in full, goals and commitments listed and shown "not yet reviewed". The 2017 deck: superseded vision, reduced review, paired. Chosen because under two years have elapsed since enactment.
- **What the AI did:** Extracted the draft annex, the 2017 deck and the 2023 keynote; obtained the enacted law and reconciled it with the draft; read the RPJMN summary and compared its paths with the vision's; validated 35 claims, 87 goal rows and 37 commitments into a register with "what would challenge it" written before evidence; built a Tier-1 source pack; gathered evidence for clusters A, B and D; ran the required-rate test on growth, GNI per capita, manufacturing share, tax ratio and CPI; ran one change-scan collection; drafted this page.
- **What people checked:** The project owner confirmed four framing decisions (scoring base and law overrides; tracks and pairing; both growth paths in the required-rate test; the BPS middle-class series) and approved each step before the next. **No rating has been reviewed by a person.**
- **Source tiers:** Ratings rest on Tier 1 only — BPS, Bank Indonesia, Kemenkeu, Bappenas, BPK, KLH; IMF Article IV and WEO, World Bank, WTO, APO, ILO. Tier 2 (Mandiri Institute, S&P Global, IESR, CAT, TI as an index) for context and change-scan trends. Media only to flag candidates and as carriers of Tier-1 statements.
- **Sources behind the change scan:** RPJMN 2025-2029 and RKP 2026-27; IMF Article IV (Jan 2026); World Bank IEP (Dec 2025, Jun 2026) and EAP Update (Apr 2026); BI LPI 2025; WTO Global Trade Outlook (Oct 2025, Oct 2026); WEF Global Risks 2026; the register's absent-topic check; developments met in the evidence passes.
- **Proxies and reviewer choices:** middle class scored on the BPS series the RPJMN uses, not the plan's own 38 → 80 % (cut-off unstated); potential growth inferred from the IMF output gap and growth path (no Tier-1 body publishes a figure); ICOR computed by the reviewer (GFCF share ÷ growth); tax ratio as computed by DDTC/Kontan from Kemenkeu and BPS figures (the plan's definition may include natural-resource non-tax revenue); CPI used because the law adopts it as indicator 27b.
- **Not reviewed:** clusters C (demography and consuming class), E (technology), F (climate and resources), G (regional): F2, F6a, F6b, F7, F8, 1.4-1.6, 1.8, 2.1-2.4, 3.3, 4.1, 5.1; 82 goal rows; all 37 commitments for delivery. 2.5 and 5.2 logged only.
- **Limits:** (i) Indonesia's 2025 Atlas GNI per capita (USD 5,120) read from Katadata and Kemenko Perekonomian accounts of the World Bank release — verify at data.worldbank.org. (ii) BPS 2026 quarterly growth and manufacturing shares taken from BPS release pages and ≥ 3 press reports; BRS PDFs not opened. (iii) Potential growth (1.2) rated on inference. (iv) APO 2025 TFP method differs from the edition the law used. (v) Manufacturing series has a 2010 classification break (N3-10). (vi) SNDC figures read from Argus, Kompas, IESR and CAT, not the UNFCCC document; the FOLU basis of the 1.03 vs 1.35-1.49 Gt comparison must be checked. (vii) BI tightening reported as 75 bp or 100 bp depending on carrier. (viii) KDMP and Danantara figures are ministerial statements; no audited data. (ix) Regulatory counts (BPK) are on a different basis from the law's. (x) One change-scan run, not two. (xi) Historical growth figures in the required-rate test's "best on record" column (1996; 2010-12) are from the reviewer's record of the BPS series, marked "verify". Full logs: cluster docs §4-5, change scan §5.
- **Method:** `claude/scenario-review-method.md` v0.2; proposals 21-23 and the notes in the handoff §7 (standing-check departures; definition drift; baseline replacement) arose from this run.
