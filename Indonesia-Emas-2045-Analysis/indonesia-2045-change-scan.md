# Indonesia Emas 2045 — change scan (N6)

17 November 2026. Method v0.2 §"Change scan". Candidates X1-X10 from `claude/indonesia-2045-review-scope.md` §5, plus X11 and X12 found during the evidence passes (clusters B and D). Evidence already gathered in the cluster docs is referenced, not repeated. Direction is judged against the vision's own goals. Findings are facts about the text and the data.

**Collection sources (fixed, decision 18):** RPJMN 2025-2029 and RKP 2026-27 (the government's own later plans); IMF Article IV Jan 2026; World Bank IEP Dec 2025 / Jun 2026 and EAP Update Apr 2026; BI LPI 2025; WTO Global Trade Outlook Oct 2025 / Oct 2026; WEF Global Risks Report 2026 (shared pack, first entry); the absent-topic check (register §7); developments met in the evidence passes. **Domains** (each returns a candidate or "none found"): demographic and social — X4, X8; economic and trade — X2, X6, X7, X11; technological — X3; environmental and climate — X9; domestic political and institutional — X1, X12; geopolitical and security — X10; fiscal and financial — X5; international rules and regulation — X2 (CBAM, ART) and X2a (CEPA). No domain returned "none found".

**One collection run only.** Decision 18 asks for two independent runs with differences sent to human review; this document is run 1. A second run should be made from the same source list without reading this one.

### X1 — New government and the RPJMN's steeper growth path, now being lowered year by year through the RKP

- **Kind:** major shift. **Domain:** domestic political and institutional. **Since:** 20 Oct 2024 (government); 10 Feb 2025 (Perpres 12/2025); Aug 2026 (RKP 2027).
- **Evidence (Tier 1):** RPJMN growth path 5.3 / 6.3 / 7.5 / 7.7 / 8.0 % for 2025-29 against the vision's 5.6-6.1 % (standing-check doc); RKP 2027 growth target set at 5.9-7.5 % in May 2026 and 6 % in the final RKP / RAPBN 2027, with 8 % for 2029 retained (cluster D, F10 row). New flagship programmes (MBG, KDMP, Danantara, food estates, 3-million houses) — X5.
- **In the report?** Continuity addressed (Mission 8, Bab VI); a path steeper than the vision's own absent.
- **Links:** F10 — opportunity; acts on the assumption; the cascade produced an RPJMN and two RKPs on schedule that quote the vision; already acting; established. F1/3.1 — both; acts on the path; the operative target for 2027 fell from 7.5 to 6 % while the 2029 end-point stayed, which raises the rate required in 2028-29 (cluster A §2a); already acting; established. F9 — challenge; acts on the means; flagship programmes draw on the fiscal space the vision assigns to tax-base expansion; already acting; established.

### X2 — US "reciprocal" tariffs and the bilateral settlement; product-specific Western barriers on downstream metals; the EU CEPA as offset (X2a)

- **Kind:** major shift. **Domain:** economic and trade; international rules. **Since:** Apr 2025 (32 % announced); Jul 2025 (19 % agreed); 1 Jan 2026 (CBAM definitive); 19 Feb 2026 (ART signed); 1 Jul 2026 (EU steel TRQ).
- **Evidence:** cluster B, F4 (implied) and F12 rows: 19 % tariff with 0 % on 1,819 lines; Indonesian tariffs removed on >99 % of US goods; US/EU/UK 50 % steel duties; CBAM on stainless; EU anti-dumping. Offset X2a: EU-Indonesia CEPA concluded Sept 2025, sent for signature 29 Jun 2026, entry into force targeted Jan 2027 as GSP ends. Tier 1 documents via Tier 2/3 carriers.
- **In the report?** Absent (no tariff or trade-war scenario; "integrasi ekonomi global" is a direction, IE7).
- **Links:** F4 (implied openness) — challenge; acts on the assumption; rules-based access replaced by negotiated terms and product barriers; already acting; established. F12 — challenge; acts on the assumption; Western markets for the roadmap's lead products (steel, stainless, nickel) carry new duties; already acting; established. 3.2 / S-3 — challenge; acts on the path; export-led manufacturing expansion faces higher landed costs in the US and EU; already acting; established. I-22 — both; acts on the goal; 2025 exports still grew 7 % and the CEPA opens the EU; already acting; established (growth) / emerging (CEPA effect). F11 — challenge; acts on the assumption; a dated discontinuity in trade policy; already acting; established.

### X3 — Generative AI: a labour-market effect of a different shape from "40 % of jobs replaced"

- **Kind:** trend. **Domain:** technological. **Since:** late 2022; ILO measurement 2025-26.
- **Evidence (Tier 1, ILO brief 2025, blog 21 Apr 2026, report Jul 2026):** ILO projections for 2025 put 3.3 percent of the ASEAN workforce (11.7 million) in the highest exposure category, with 67 percent entirely unexposed; Indonesia shows 21.7 percent exposure; only 3 to 4 per cent of jobs in Indonesia, the Philippines and Thailand fall into the highest GenAI exposure category, and GenAI exposure among clerical support workers in Indonesia is 93.9 per cent. Employment in the occupations with the greatest automation potential has continued to grow since 2017, including after generative AI emerged. Trade side: AI-enabling goods supplied 42-47 % of world merchandise trade growth in 2025-26 (X11).
- **In the report?** Mentioned in passing: "disruption", 40 % of jobs (Gambar 2.1.1, p. 31); AI listed among technologies to master (p. 104). Generative AI as a distinct force absent.
- **Links:** F7 — both; acts on the assumption; displacement so far is far below the 40 % framing and concentrated in clerical work, while the "manageable through skills" half is untested; already acting; established (exposure) / emerging (effects). F3 — opportunity; acts on the means; AI is a productivity instrument the plan's TFP lever did not name; expected within the horizon; emerging. I-15b / I-16 — unclear; acts on the goal; clerical and female-dominated occupations carry the highest exposure (ILO gender finding); expected; emerging.

### X4 — Decline of the BPS middle class since 2019

- **Kind:** trend. **Domain:** demographic and social. **Since:** 2019-20 (COVID), continuing through 2025.
- **Evidence (Tier 1 data, Tier 2 processing):** BPS 2024: middle class 17.13 % (47.85 m). Mandiri Institute, using BPS's 2025 Susenas, records 1.2 million people leaving the middle class in 2025, from 47.9 million in 2024 to 46.7 million, while the aspiring middle class rose. BPS data show about 7.13 million middle-class people lost economic status over 2020-2025 (via commentary citing BPS). Middle-class consumption growth 4.1 % in 2025, lowest of the groups (same source). RPJMN target: 17.50 % (2025) → 20.00 % (2029).
- **In the report?** Absent (the plan projects 38 → 80 % on an unstated cut-off; register N3-5).
- **Links:** F6b / 3.3 — challenge; acts on the assumption; the series the government uses has fallen for six years while the plan assumes a rise; already acting; established (Tier 2 with data; BPS's own 2025 figure pending). S-4 / S-5 — unclear; acts on the path; poverty fell to 8.07 % while the middle thinned (BPS Mar 2026, cluster A source pack) — distribution moving at both ends; already acting; established.

### X5 — New recurrent programmes and state vehicles not in the vision: MBG, Danantara, Koperasi Desa Merah Putih, food estates, housing

- **Kind:** major shift. **Domain:** fiscal and financial. **Since:** Jan 2025 (MBG); 24 Feb 2025 (Danantara); Inpres 17/2025 and PMK 49/2025 (KDMP); 2025 (Lumbung Pangan, 3-million houses).
- **Evidence (Tier 1 via carriers):** MBG — Rp 51.5 tn realised 2025; 2026 ceiling Rp 335 tn cut to Rp 268 tn; Rp 153.2 tn to Sept 2026 for 57 m recipients (cluster D). Danantara — Rp 120 tn state capital; SOE dividends Rp 142.3 tn in 2025; consolidated accounts pending (cluster D). KDMP (ministry and Bakom statements carried by Antara, Kompas, Tempo; Tier 1 statements, Tier 3 carriers) — 82,000 cooperatives are to receive Himbara loans of up to Rp 240 trillion under PMK 49/2025; about Rp 40 trillion of the Rp 60.6 trillion Dana Desa 2026 goes to repaying the Kopdes loans over six years; Kemenkeu disbursed Rp 90 trillion of capital spending in Q1 2026 toward 30,000 KDMP; 10,070 KDKMP were operating by 15 October 2026, with 85,427 transactions worth Rp 208.64 billion recorded in 2026, against a target of 40,000 operating by end-2026.
- **In the report?** Absent in the RPJPN; Danantara present in the RPJMN as a proposed law; MBG, KDMP, food estates present in the RPJMN.
- **Links:** F9 — challenge; acts on the assumption; a recurrent line of ≈ 1 % of GDP plus contingent liabilities (Rp 240 tn of guaranteed loans serviced from Dana Desa) occupy space the vision assigns to tax-base expansion; already acting; established. 6.1 — both; acts on the means; Danantara is a new non-APBN channel, but its contribution is unaudited; already acting; emerging. F13 — challenge; acts on the means; delivery capacity is being spent on programmes outside the 20 game changers (10,070 of 80,000 KDMP operating; MBG ceiling cut mid-year); already acting; established. GC-5 (poverty, Regsosek) and GC-4 (stunting) — opportunity; acts on the goal; MBG reaches 57-63 m people with a nutrition objective; already acting; emerging (no outcome data).

### X6 — Nickel: record export values, single-market dependence, and state supply management as prices fell

- **Kind:** trend. **Domain:** economic and trade. **Since:** 2024-26.
- **Evidence:** cluster B, F12 row (BPS trade data, ESDM quota decisions via Tier 2/3 carriers): HS 75 exports at a record in 2025 and +50 % Jan-Aug 2026; 92.6 % of HS 75 exports to China; 2026 ore quota cut from 379 Mt (approved 2025) to 260-270 Mt, revised to 290-300 Mt; further 2027 cuts signalled; EU CBAM/AD/safeguards on stainless.
- **In the report?** Mentioned: nickel downstreaming as achievement (p. 13) and roadmap input (p. 96); concentration and price management absent.
- **Links:** F12 — challenge; acts on the assumption; "access from all sides" does not describe a value chain selling nine-tenths to one market; already acting; established. GC-7 / 3.2 — both; acts on the means; the downstreaming engine works (value up) but now runs under quota management and Western carbon/trade duties; already acting; established. 2.4 — opportunity; acts on the assumption; Indonesia holds ≈ 60 % of mined nickel and is using that position; already acting; established.

### X8 — SUPAS 2025: the dependency ratio is rising earlier than the plan's projection

- **Kind:** trend. **Domain:** demographic. **Since:** published 5 May 2026.
- **Evidence (Tier 1, BPS SUPAS 2025, source pack):** population 284.67 m (projection 285.2 m); dependency ratio **45.05** against the BPS 2020-2050 projection of 44.02 for 2025 (databoks citing BPS, source pack) and the plan's 44.3 (2020) → 48.3 (2035); TFR 2.13; 65+ 11.97 %.
- **In the report?** Addressed (Grafik 2.2.1, "about 15 years" of dependency ratio below 50 %).
- **Links:** F2 — challenge; acts on the assumption; the ratio is one point above the projected path in 2025, so the 50 % crossing moves earlier unless fertility and ageing paths change; already acting; established (one survey point; BPS's revised projection pending). 2.1 — unclear; acts on the assumption; population close to projection; established.

### X9 — Second NDC (27 Oct 2025) and the 2023 inventory: the government's own emissions path sits above the vision's green-economy path

- **Kind:** major shift (dated submission) on an established trend. **Domain:** environmental and climate. **Since:** 27 Oct 2025.
- **Evidence:** KLH inventory: actual emissions 1,360.35 MtCO₂e in 2023 (source pack, Tier 1). SNDC (Tier 1 document, read through Argus, Kompas, IESR, CAT accounts): absolute caps based on one scenario assuming 6 % growth in 2030 and 6.7 % in 2035, and another assuming 7 % and 8.3 %; 2030 emissions of 1.346 bn t CO₂e under the first and 1.491 bn t under the second, against a 1.145 bn t baseline in 2019, the reference year; the energy sector is projected to peak in 2038, so emissions do not peak by 2035; the renewable-energy mix target was reduced to 17-19 % by 2025 from 23 %. Vision path (Grafik 4.5.1, law p. 129): 1.50 Gt (2025) → 1.03 Gt (2030) → 0.55 Gt (2045); renewables 20 % (2025) → 70 % (2045); RPJMN 23 % (2029).
- **In the report?** Addressed (NZE 2060, green-economy scenario); a later NDC with a higher 2030 level absent.
- **Links:** F8 / 4.1 — challenge; acts on the goal and the path; the NDC's 2030 level (1.35-1.49 Gt) is 0.3-0.5 Gt above the vision's 1.03 Gt for the same year, and the energy peak (2038) comes after the vision's phase-2 decline; already acting; established (subject to the basis check, N3-1 and the RPJMN's different series). I-18b — challenge; acts on the goal; the 2025 renewables figure was lowered below the vision's 20 % baseline; established. S-9 — unclear; acts on the goal; intensity (not level) targets may still be met on a higher GDP path; emerging.

### X10 — Middle East conflict of 2026: oil-price shock, rupiah pressure, monetary tightening

- **Kind:** major shift. **Domain:** geopolitical and security (with fiscal and financial effects). **Since:** late Feb 2026.
- **Evidence:** the US-Israel and Iran conflict and the temporary closure of the Strait of Hormuz triggered what the IEA called the largest oil supply disruption in the history of the market, with Bank Indonesia delivering a cumulative 100 bp of tightening (KB Valbury, Tier 2, citing IEA); the 2026 fuel subsidy was premised on USD 70 a barrel, Indonesia imports a fifth to a quarter of its oil from the Middle East, and the deficit must stay within 3 % of GDP (Kemenko Perekonomian via AFP); the World Bank's April 2026 EAP Update cut Indonesia's 2026 growth projection to 4.7 %, while the disruption raised demand for Indonesian coal and CPO; BI held at 4.75 % through April and then raised the rate by a cumulative 75 bp from May to 5.5 % (LPEM UI via Jakarta Globe); the rupiah traded at Rp 17,300-17,900 per dollar in April-June 2026. WTO: sustained high oil prices could cut 0.5 pt from 2026 trade growth (cluster B).
- **In the report?** Absent (no external-shock case; F11 implied).
- **Links:** F11 — challenge; acts on the assumption; a second dated discontinuity in the first two years of the plan; already acting; established. F9 — challenge; acts on the means; subsidy cost rises with every dollar on oil while the deficit sits at 2.9 % of GDP; already acting; established. F1 — challenge; acts on the path; forecasts for 2026 were cut to 4.7-5.0 % in April-June 2026 (outturn to Q3 was 5.46 %, so the effect was smaller than forecast); already acting; established. F8 — both; acts on the means; higher coal and CPO demand raises export income and emissions; already acting; emerging. F12 (finance) — unclear; sovereign access intact, rating agencies flag exposure; emerging.

## 3. Gap lists (step 5) — stated as facts about the text

**Challenges for which the vision contains no commitment**
- Tariff or trade-policy shocks from major partners (X2): the vision has a direction (IE7) and a roadmap sequence to "net export", but no measure for negotiated market access or contingency for partner tariffs.
- External energy-price shocks and their fiscal transmission (X10): GC-17 (subsidy reform) addresses the structure, not the shock; no stabilisation rule or buffer is named.
- A falling middle class (X4): IE3 adaptive social protection targets poverty; no measure addresses the middle of the distribution.
- An NDC path above the plan's own GHG path (X9): the vision contains the path, not a mechanism for reconciling it with later NDCs.
- Single-market dependence of the downstreaming engine (X6): the roadmap sequences products, not markets.

**Opportunities the vision's commitments do not use**
- AI-enabling goods as the trade driver (X11) and generative AI as a productivity instrument (X3): GC-9 (digital superplatform, digital talent) and the electronics line in Tabel 4.2.1 are the nearest commitments; neither names AI hardware or AI adoption as a lever.
- The EU CEPA (X2a) and the end of GSP: not named; the 2035-39 "global expansion" phase has no agreement list.
- Danantara as a consolidated SOE-investment channel (X5): the RPJMN uses it; the vision's 6.2 names SOEs and blended finance generically.
- The 2026 commodity windfall (coal, CPO) from X10: not a commitment; noted because it pulls against F8.
