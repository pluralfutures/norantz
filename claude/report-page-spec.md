# Norantz report page: spec sheet

Version 0.2, 16 November 2026. Draft for review.

This spec describes the public page for one reviewed report: which sections it has, what goes in each, and why each is there. It has two companions:

- `report-page-template.md` is the fill-in skeleton for the page.
- `scenario-review-method.md` describes how the content is produced.

**What changed from version 0.1**

- The page now serves two kinds of report: visions (the normative track in method v0.2) and scenario sets (the exploratory track). The skeleton is the same for both. What goes in each section differs, and the spec says how.
- A new section 10, "What has changed since publication", carries the change scan: the emerging issues, trends and major shifts since the report came out, shown as challenges and opportunities and tied to the assumptions they bear on.
- Sections 10 to 16 of version 0.1 are now 11 to 17. A number map is at the end.
- The header shows the report's stance and whether it is still in force.
- Goals and commitments get their own status labels, separate from the rating of assumptions.
- A third reader, the policymaker or adviser, is added.

The template and the España 2050 page draft still follow version 0.1 and are now out of step with this spec.

The spec assumes the proposed defaults for open decisions 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 and 20 in the method. None of those is agreed. Sections that depend on one are marked.

## Who the page is for

| Reader | What they want | How they read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General reader, educated but not a specialist (a junior consultant is the reference point) | What the report was, why it mattered, where things stand now | The top of the page and one or two sections. Often on a phone. |
| Policymaker or adviser | What is new since the report, which assumptions are under pressure, which recommendations to revisit | The top of the page, then sections 10 and 12. |
| Policy researcher | The assumptions, the evidence behind each rating, the sources, something to cite | Down to the register, then the download. |

## Design principles

1. **Then and now, side by side.** Every section about the report's content carries a reading of where it stands today. A reader should never have to scroll between "what the report said" and "what has happened".
2. **Describe, do not grade.** Scenarios explore possibilities; they are not forecasts. Visions state aims; they are not forecasts either. The page reports what the evidence shows about a report's assumptions and where the figures stand against its goals. It does not say whether the authors were right, whether the goals were the right ones, or how a government has performed. There is no overall score.
3. **Three layers.** A one-minute summary, the findings page, and the full register behind it. Each layer is complete on its own.
4. **Every claim is dated and sourced.** Findings carry an "as of" date and a link to the source.
5. **Show what was not checked.** Untested themes appear as "not yet reviewed". They are not left out.
6. **One skeleton for every report.** Sections stay in the same order on every page, for visions and scenario sets alike, so readers can compare countries. A section that does not apply keeps its heading and says why in one line.
7. **Challenges and opportunities get equal weight.** In section 10 neither list is longer by design, placed first by default, or collapsed while the other is open. Both are judged against the report's own goals, not the reviewer's view of what is good.
8. **Looking back and looking ahead are kept apart.** A rating says what the evidence shows so far. A pressure reading says what new conditions may mean from here. The page never blends the two into one label.

## How the two tracks share one page

| Report type | Plain label on the page | What the page centres on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normative | "A vision: one desired future and a route to it" | Sections 9 (targets), 10 (what has changed) and 12 (recommendations) |
| Hybrid | "A vision with comparison scenarios" | As for a vision, with the other futures shown as comparison paths in sections 5 and 8 |
| Exploratory | "A scenario set: several possible futures, none preferred" | Sections 6 (drivers), 8 (signposts) and 10 (range) |

## Sections

"Fed by" names the stage of the review method that supplies the content. N stages are the normative track, E stages the exploratory track. "Gap" means no stage supplies it yet.

### Part A. Orientation

| # | Section | What it contains | Why it is there | Fed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Header | Title, country, publisher, publication year, horizon year, the plain type label, the force it had when published (law, executive order, adopted policy, advisory, non-government), its status today (in force, revised, superseded, lapsed), "reviewed as of" date, version, share of themes reviewed, next review date, language switch, link to the original. A superseded or lapsed report carries a one-line banner naming its successor. | Tells the reader in five seconds what they are looking at, whether anyone is still acting on it, and how fresh the review is. The coverage figure stops a partial review reading as a complete one. | N1 or E1. Decisions 6, 12, 13, 17. |
| 1 | At a glance | A summary of up to 120 words and three findings. For a vision, at least one finding comes from the change scan. A tally of assumptions by rating and, for a vision, of goals by status, shown next to the coverage figure. | This is the whole page for most general readers. It must stand alone when shared or quoted. The tallies give the shape of the review without implying a grade. | N8 or E7. Decision 11. |
| 2 | How to read this page | Fixed text, with one paragraph that differs by track. For a vision: goals are the authors' aims, not forecasts; "behind" describes a figure, not a government; a challenge or opportunity is judged against the vision's own goals. For a scenario set: scenarios are not predictions and none is expected to "come true"; the page looks at whether the drivers mattered and whether events stayed inside the range. Both: what each label means, and what this report type allows us to test. | Sets expectations before any label is seen. Without it, "weakened" reads as "the authors were wrong" and "behind" as "the government failed". | Fixed text, plus classification from stage 1. Decisions 8, 19. |

### Part B. The report (then)

| # | Section | What it contains | Why it is there | Fed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Why it was written | Who commissioned and wrote it, the political and economic moment, how it was made (expert panel, public consultation, joint work with an outside body), and who was consulted. | A report answers the worries of its time. Readers cannot judge its assumptions without knowing what the authors were looking at and who was in the room. | Partly N1 or E1 (how it was made). Gap for commissioner and moment. |
| 4 | The questions it explored | The decision questions or national challenges the report set out to address, each with a status today: still central, still relevant but changed shape, or overtaken by events. | Visitors asked for this directly: are these still the right questions? A report can have sound assumptions about a question that no longer matters, or the reverse. | Gap for the questions. For a vision, the status can draw on change-scan links that act on the goal itself (N6). |
| 5 | The futures it set out | One card per future: name, a two-sentence story, what the country looks like at the horizon year, and the conditions it needs. **Vision:** one card for the desired future and, where the report gives one, one for the "without action" case. **Hybrid:** one card per future, with the preferred one labelled "the report's goal". **Scenario set:** one card per scenario, equal in size. A line on how the futures relate. | Readers need the futures in plain words before any comparison with events makes sense. Equal cards for a scenario set stop one looking like the "official" one. For a vision the label is a fact about the text, so it is shown. | N1 or E1. The stories need a summary step, checked against the report's wording. |
| 6 | What the futures depend on | **Vision:** the vision's logic as a short chain: what it aims for, what it says will be done, and what the outside world must provide. Each link carries a one-word status and points to section 7, 9 or 12 for the evidence. **Scenario set:** the forces that separate one scenario from another, each with what the report expected, what the evidence shows now, and a rating. | This is where current events meet the report. For a vision it shows the reader, in one view, where the route could break. | Vision: N2 (assumption checklist, item classes). Scenario set: E2 and the driver test in E6. |

### Part C. The review (now)

| # | Section | What it contains | Why it is there | Fed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | Key assumptions | The ranked themes, each with its main assumption, whether it was stated or implied, how much of the report depends on it, the evidence in one line, and two readings kept apart: a rating so far, and for a vision a pressure reading (under pressure, supported, mixed, watch, untouched) that names the strongest change and links to it in section 10. Link to the full register. | A few assumptions carry most of a report. Ranking by dependency shows which findings matter. The pressure reading shows which assumptions new conditions bear on, even where the data has not yet moved. | N2, N4, N5, N6, N7 or E2, E4, E5, E6. Decisions 2, 3, 6. |
| 8 | Where things stand | **Vision:** a short reading by goal area: on the path, below it or above it, and whether the goal is still reachable. For a hybrid, the comparison paths are shown beside the preferred one. **Scenario set:** which signposts can be observed, scenario by scenario and element by element. **Both:** if the country is on none of the report's paths, a neutral description of the observed path and a statement that the report did not model it. | This answers "which future are we in?", the question most visitors arrive with. Reading by element avoids a single verdict the evidence may not support. | N8 (path reading) or E6 (signpost test). Decision 4. |
| 9 | Targets | **Quantified vision:** each target, its path, the latest figure, the gap, a status (met, ahead, on path, behind), and the required-rate finding in one plain sentence. The figure and the gap come before the label. **Qualitative vision:** the proxy indicators, each labelled as chosen by the reviewer. **Superseded vision:** the last known status, marked "no longer in force". **Scenario set:** a one-line note that the report sets no targets. | Targets are the most checkable part of a vision and the easiest for a general reader to grasp. For a quantified vision this is the main section. | N5, N7. Decisions 1, 16, 17, 19. |
| 10 | What has changed since publication | **Vision:** up to twelve changes. Each shows what kind it is (major shift, trend or emerging issue), since when, the evidence in one line, whether the report addressed it (addressed, mentioned in passing, absent), and the assumptions, goals or measures it bears on, with one sentence on how. Changes are grouped as challenges and opportunities, with those that cut both ways shown in both. Emerging issues are marked as less certain. Two short lists follow: challenges the vision has no measure for, and opportunities its measures do not use. **Scenario set:** the same list of changes, each read as inside the range the scenarios spanned, at the edge, or outside it. | This answers "is this vision still fit for the world it now faces?" It is the forward-looking half of the page and the section a policymaker reads first. Tying each change to a named assumption keeps it from becoming a general trends list. | N6 (change scan), or the coverage test in E6. Decisions 18, 20. |
| 11 | What the report did not cover | Topics absent from the report at the time it was written that have since mattered. Wording stays factual: "the report does not discuss". Later developments are in section 10; items that appear in both are cross-linked. | Omissions are assumptions. The pilot's unstated assumption of a calm external environment was among its largest findings. | N2 or E2 (absent-topic check). |
| 12 | Recommendations | Groups of recommendations, each marked still stands, revisit, revisit: new conditions, or not assessed, with the assumptions it rests on. For a vision, each group also shows what was done: delivered, partly delivered, not delivered, dropped or not assessed. For a scenario set, the report's implications, or a one-line note if it gives none. | This is the practical output for policymakers. "Revisit: new conditions" flags advice whose footing has shifted before the data shows it. Tying each status to named assumptions keeps the most subjective step traceable. | N8 or E6 (implications test). Decision 5. |
| 13 | What to watch next | Three to five indicators, each with the level that would change a rating and when the next figure is due. Emerging issues with a "watch" reading in section 10 are candidates. | Turns a backward-looking review into something readers can use. It also commits the site to saying in advance what would change its mind. | "What would challenge it" from stage 2, and N6. Decisions 1 and 13. |

### Part D. The record

| # | Section | What it contains | Why it is there | Fed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14 | What happened to the report | The fuller story behind the header status: whether it was adopted, cited in law or budgets, updated, replaced or set aside, and by what. | A report that shaped policy and one that was shelved deserve different readings. Researchers ask this first. | N1 (standing check). Still a gap on the exploratory track. |
| 15 | How this review was done | The track used and why, what the AI did, what people checked, the source tiers used, the sources behind the change scan, every proxy and signpost the reviewer chose, the themes not reviewed, and the limits log. Link to the full method. | The findings are produced by AI at scale, so readers need to see the process and its limits before they trust or cite it. | N9 or E8, plus the human checkpoints of every stage. Decisions 7, 16, 18. |
| 16 | Response from the authors | A reply from the report's authors or publisher, unedited, or a note that one was invited. | Supports neutrality and the non-critical tone. It also catches errors the review missed. | Gap. A process, not a pipeline stage. |
| 17 | Sources, versions and reuse | Source list, version history with dates, citation text, data download, related reports by theme, and a link to report an error. | Researchers need to cite a fixed version and get the data. Version history shows how findings moved between reviews. | N5, N9 or E5, E8. Decisions 10 and 13. |

## A superseded or lapsed vision

The page keeps the full skeleton (principle 6) with these differences:

- The header banner names the successor and links to its page if one exists.
- Sections 7, 10 and 11 are filled as normal. Assumptions and changes in the environment often carry into the successor.
- Section 9 shows each target's last known status, marked "no longer in force".
- Section 12 shows commitments as "not assessed".
- Section 14 moves up to sit directly after section 2, since it is the first thing the reader needs.

## Open questions for the page

| # | Question | Why it matters | Suggested default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Should the public page use the label "Invalidated"? | It may read as a verdict on the authors. | Keep the scale in the register. Test a softer public label such as "Not supported by evidence so far". |
| 2 | Should section 8 name a single closest scenario? | A single call is clearer but hides mixed evidence. | Read by element. Name a closest scenario only when most elements agree. |
| 3 | Do the tallies in section 1 work as a score? | Readers and media may quote "x of y targets missed". The risk is higher for a vision than for a scenario set. | Show counts by label, never a percentage or a grade, and always next to coverage. |
| 4 | How long is the page on a phone? | Eighteen sections is a long scroll. | Sections 1 and 2 open; the rest collapsed under their headings, with jump links. For a vision, section 10 opens too. |
| 5 | Which status scale for section 4? | The three levels proposed are untested. | Trial them on España 2050 and one ASEAN vision. |
| 6 | How many labels can one assumption carry in section 7? | A rating, a horizon view and a pressure reading are three labels on one line. | Show two on the page: the rating so far and the pressure reading. Keep the "plausible by the horizon year" view in the register for assumptions, and show reachability for goals in section 9. This narrows how decision 2 appears in public. |
| 7 | Do sections 6 and 7 repeat each other for a vision? | Both deal with what the vision rests on. | Section 6 is the logic in one view with one-word statuses. Section 7 carries the evidence. Test with readers; merge if they see no difference. |
| 8 | Does the challenges list in section 10 read as criticism? | A list of what the vision did not foresee can look like a charge sheet, however it is worded. | Keep "in the report?" as a plain fact. Always show opportunities beside challenges. Lead each entry with the change, not with the vision's gap. |
| 9 | How are challenges and opportunities laid out on a phone? | Side by side does not fit a narrow screen, and stacking puts one list first. | Two tabs of equal size, with the tab shown first alternating by report or chosen by which list is longer. Test with readers. |
| 10 | Does a superseded vision get a full page or a short one? | A full skeleton with many "not assessed" lines may frustrate readers. A short page breaks principle 6. | Full skeleton with the banner and section 14 moved up, as set out above. Revisit after the first such page. |
| 11 | What does a general reader call a "normative" report? | The method's terms are specialist. | Use "vision" and "scenario set" on the page. Keep "normative" and "exploratory" for the method and the register. |

## Known limits of this spec

- It is designed from one pilot. No reader has tested it.
- No change scan has been run, so section 10 is specified without a worked example. Its size, layout and tone are the least certain parts of this version.
- The scenario-set variants rest on a track that has not been run on any report.
- Section order reflects a judgement about what each reader wants first. Researchers may prefer the register higher; policymakers may prefer section 10 higher.
- The template and the España 2050 page draft have not been updated to this version.
- It covers the page for one report only. The home page, the country and theme indexes, and the cross-report comparison view are not specified.
