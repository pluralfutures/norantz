# Indonesia Emas 2045 — three-document intake analysis

Draft, 17 November 2026. Covers the three Indonesian documents in the project: the 2017 "Visi Indonesia 2045" oration deck, the May 2023 Musrenbangnas keynote, and the Rancangan Akhir RPJPN 2025-2045 annex (the "legal document"). Five questions per document: normative future, key issues, key assumptions, publication date (best guess), and the time period in focus. A cross-document section at the end records what moved between vintages, which is what Norantz will want to show.

Sources: `Indonesia-Bappenas-Visi-2045-Oration-2017/slides.md`, `Indonesia-Bappenas-Emas-2045-Keynote/chunks.jsonl`, `Indonesia-Bappenas-RPJPN-2025-2045-Draft/part1-figures.json` and READMEs. Page/slide references are to those extractions. Figures marked "~" were read off charts, not printed.

## 1. Visi Indonesia 2045 — oration deck (Bappenas, 2017)

### Publication date and time period

- **Date:** 26 September 2017, printed on the cover (Orasi Ilmiah, FEB Universitas Indonesia). Not a guess. The file name says 25 September; the slide says 26.
- **Data vintage:** 2013-2017. GERD 2013, Riskesdas 2013, farm census 2013, RUEN 2016, UN WPP 2015, PwC "World in 2050" (2017), GCI 2016-17. The poverty and Gini charts start in 2017.
- **Period in focus:** **2016-2045**, in three ten-year phases (2016-25 structural strengthening, 2026-35 innovation-led acceleration, 2036-45 quality-based modernisation). Two longer tails: the culture roadmap and "Impian Indonesia" run to **2085**, and energy targets follow RUEN to **2050**. Historical comparison window is 1986-2015.
- **Standing:** advisory/draft-stage ministerial presentation. No legal force. It is the pre-history of the vision later enacted as RPJPN 2025-2045.

### Normative future

Vision wording: **"Berdaulat, Maju, Adil dan Makmur"** (sovereign, advanced, just and prosperous), anchored in the 1945 Constitution preamble and President Jokowi's handwritten "Impian Indonesia 2015-2085" (slide 3). Four pillars on a Pancasila base (slide 4): human capital and S&T; sustainable economic development; equitable development; national resilience and governance.

The desired end state is quantified mainly through the **high scenario** (slides 9-10):

| Quantity | 2045 end state (high scenario) |
| --- | --- |
| Average growth 2016-45 | 6.4 %/yr (phases 6 / 7 / 6.3) |
| High-income status | 2035; MIT exit 2034 |
| GDP per capita | USD 28,934 |
| World GDP rank | #4 (MER and PPP) |
| Investment / GDP | 39 % |
| Industry / GDP | 32 % (growth 7.8-8.1 %/yr) |
| Openness (X+M)/GDP | 54 %; export share 2.0 %, rank 10 |
| Poverty / Gini | 0.02 % / ~0.34 with redistribution |
| KTI share of economy | 25.1 % (from 19.5 % in 2015) |
| Education | 12 years mean schooling by 2026-35; tertiary GER 60 %; 90 % of workforce secondary+ |
| R&D | 1.5-2.0 % of GDP |
| Tourism / maritime | 85 m foreign tourists; maritime sectors 30 % of GDP |
| Energy | renewables 23 % (2025), 31 % (2050); nuclear kept as option |
| Emissions | -41 % vs baseline by 2030 (NDC conditional) |
| Governance | three-decade bureaucratic reform path; "anti-corruption society" by 2045; substantive democracy |

Stance for the method: **hybrid**. A baseline/BAU scenario (5.1 % growth, rank 8, USD 19,794) is shown as the "without reform or with a weak world economy" case; the high scenario is the one argued for. The baseline is a comparison path, not an alternative future the authors are neutral about.

### Key issues the deck focuses on

1. **Demographic window.** Dependency ratio bottoms at 46.9 % in 2028-2031, then rises; population 318.7 m and 69.1 % urban by 2045; 65+ rises from 11.9 m to 42.8 m (slide 7). The bonus has to be used before it closes.
2. **Java concentration and urbanisation.** Java 55-59 % of population; Jakarta-Bandung mega-region of 76 m by 2035 (slide 8).
3. **Structural transformation.** Reindustrialisation to 32 % of GDP; shift of exports from commodities to manufactures and services (slides 9, 26, 31).
4. **Human capital gaps.** Only ~9,000 of 750,000 engineers work as professional engineers; GERD 0.08 % of GDP vs 4.14 % Korea; GCI rank 37 (slides 14-15).
5. **Health transition.** NCDs 37 % → 57 % of disease burden 1990-2015; stunting 37.2 %, adult obesity 15.4 %, maternal anaemia 37.1 % (slide 17).
6. **Infrastructure and its financing.** Government 35 %, SOEs 25 %, PPP 25 %, private 15 % (slide 22).
7. **Energy security.** Oil 46 % → 20 % of the mix by 2050; coal stays 25-30 % (slide 23).
8. **Regional inequality.** KTI share barely moved in 30 years (17.5 % → 19.5 %); affirmative allocation and higher growth targets for Maluku (9.0 %), Papua (8.3 %), Sulawesi (7.4 %) vs Java (6.1 %) (slides 37-38).
9. **Farmer welfare.** 46.2 % of the poorest 40 % are farmers; 14.6 m households on <0.5 ha (slide 36).
10. **Governance.** Bureaucratic reform in three decades, legal culture, anti-corruption milestones for 2025/2035/2045 (slides 44-45).

### Key assumptions behind the vision

Stated:

- **A1.** "Structural reform proceeds as hoped, and world growth is relatively high" is the explicit condition of the high scenario (slide 9). The baseline is defined by global shocks or low world growth.
- **A2.** Growth is front-loaded: 5.5 % for 2015-20 and 6.5 % for 2020-25, then 7 % for a decade (slide 10). The per-capita path from USD 3,377 to 28,934 depends on it.
- **A3.** Investment rises to 39 % of GDP and exports grow 7.9 %/yr with openness nearly doubling to 54 % (slide 9) — an open world trading system is taken as given.
- **A4.** Industry's share of GDP rises from 21 % to 32 % — a reversal of the deindustrialisation already visible in 2017.
- **A5.** Growth is inclusive across all income deciles; that is what drives poverty to 0.02 % (slide 34).
- **A6.** Redistribution policy works; without it the Gini climbs to ~0.44 (slide 35, Kuznets framing).
- **A7.** 65 % of infrastructure is financed outside the state budget (slide 22).
- **A8.** Population and urbanisation follow the 2010-2045 projection and UN WPP; the dependency-ratio trough is 2028-31 (slide 7).
- **A9.** Energy follows RUEN 2016: supply grows to >1,000 MTOE by 2050 with coal still a quarter of the mix (slide 23).
- **A10.** Borrowed projections hold: PwC 2017 on GDP rankings, WHO 2012 on causes of death, UN on population (structured_data.source fields).

Implied (needed by the argument, not said):

- **A11.** No large external shock between 2016 and 2045. The path runs in a straight line from 2015. (COVID-19 later broke A2 in the first five years.)
- **A12.** Policy continuity across the elections of 2019, 2024, 2029 and beyond; the deck has no continuity mechanism.
- **A13.** The state can deliver the reform agenda (merit system, e-government, deregulation, land reform) on the stated decade timetable.
- **A14.** Commodity prices are not the engine: the high scenario is defined against commodity dependence, so it assumes manufacturing and services can replace them.

## 2. Keynote "Indonesia Emas 2045" — Musrenbangnas (Bappenas, May 2023)

### Publication date and time period

- **Date:** 22 May 2023, Bali, printed on the cover and closing slide. Delivered to the national planning conference convened to prepare RPJPN 2025-2045. Every slide says "Sumber: Rancangan Awal RPJPN 2025-2045" — it is a presentation of the **initial draft**, not the final one.
- **Data vintage:** through 2022, with a few 2023 items (Global Power Index rank 34 "(2023)"; the Maluku thematic map "Bappenas 2023"; growth diagnostics "2022 update").
- **Period in focus:** **2025-2045**, four five-year phases aligned to RPJMN 1-4 (2025-29 foundations; 2030-34 acceleration; 2035-39 global expansion; 2040-45 realisation). It looks back over 2014-2022 (achievements) and 2005-2022 (RPJPN 2005-2025 scorecard, appendix slide 26). The MIT clock runs 1993-2022 (30 years in), with 22 years left.
- **Standing:** ministerial keynote; advisory. Watermarked "Tidak untuk Dikutip".
- **Audience:** regional governments. A third of the deck (slides 15-21) is about regional diagnostics and making RPJPD/RPJMD conform to the national plan.

### Normative future

Vision wording: **"Negara Nusantara Berdaulat, Maju, dan Berkelanjutan"** (slide 6) — an archipelagic state, resilient and a world maritime axis. "Adil dan Makmur" from 2017 is absorbed as attributes of Maju ("berdaya, modern, tangguh, inovatif, adil"); sustainability becomes a top-level pillar.

Structure: the "8/17/45 grand strategy" — 5 vision targets, 8 missions, 17 directions (IE1-IE17, presented as the continuation of the 17 SDGs), 45 headline indicators (slides 7-10).

Five targets with 2025 baselines (slide 8, initial-draft values):

| Target | 2025 | 2045 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GNI per capita (USD) | 5,550 | 30,300 |
| Maritime GDP share | 7.6 % | 17.5 % |
| Industry GDP share | 18.7 % | 28.0 % |
| Poverty | 6.5-7.5 % | 0.5-0.8 % |
| Gini | 0.379-0.382 (2022) | 0.290-0.320 |
| KTI share of GDP | 20.6 % (2022) | 26.0 % |
| Global Power Index | 34 (2023) | top 15 |
| Human Capital Index | 0.54 (2022) | 0.73 |
| GHG intensity reduction | 38.6 % | 93.5 % |

Other end states: nominal GDP USD 9.8 tn and 5th in the world at 7 % growth; middle class 80 % of population; manufacturing 28 % of GDP and 25.2 % of jobs; ICOR 4.6; stunting <5 %, TB and leprosy eliminated, life expectancy 80; mean schooling 12 years; 67.88 % of educated workers in skilled jobs; zero corruption, single salary system, digital public services (slides 11-13).

Stance: **normative**, one desired future. The two growth paths (6 % → MIT exit 2041; 7 % → 2038) are routes to the same end state, with the 2045 figures computed on the 7 % path.

### Key issues the keynote focuses on

1. **The middle-income trap.** 30 years in MIT (1993-2022); only 22 years left to exit; "productivity-led growth" named as the exit route (slide 12).
2. **Reform is not enough.** "From Re-Form to Trans-Form" (slide 5) — the central rhetorical claim; the method of the last two decades is judged insufficient.
3. **Human-capital deficits.** Maternal mortality 189/100,000; stunting 21.6 %; TB burden 2nd and leprosy 3rd in the world; unequal access to quality education; poverty reduction slowing; fragmented social protection (slide 11).
4. **Governance.** Regulatory and bureaucratic inefficiency, persistent corruption, weak public services, limited civil-society participation (slide 13).
5. **Regional inequality.** 20 of 34 provinces at lower-middle-income GRDP per capita; NTT USD 1,463 vs Jakarta 20,103 (slide 15). Each region has a different binding constraint (growth diagnostics, slides 16-18): connectivity in Maluku, health in Banten/West Java, education in Central Java, labour in East Java, financing in Jakarta/Bali.
6. **Central-regional coherence.** RPJPD must copy the vision, the 8-agenda structure and the 2025-2045 period; RPJMD must follow the RPJMN cycle (slides 19-21).
7. **Global megatrends** as frame (slide 3): developing economies 71 % of output; middle class 8.8 bn; trade +3.4 %/yr; 40 % of jobs replaced; population 9.45 bn; multi-currency finance; space.
8. **Achievements as capital** (slide 2): EODB 103 → 73; stunting 29 → 21.6 %; nickel downstreaming USD 0.8 → 6 bn; tourists 9.44 → 16.11 m; one-price fuel; IKN; 85.6 m land certificates.

### Key assumptions behind the vision

Stated:

- **B1.** 6-7 % average growth for 20 years is attainable; 6 % exits MIT in 2041, 7 % in 2038 (slide 12). The high-income threshold is itself projected to keep rising.
- **B2.** Growth becomes productivity-led (TFP), not input-led; investment grows 6.8 %/yr with ICOR improving to 4.6.
- **B3.** The demographic bonus is still open: working-age share falls only slowly, 69.3 % (2020) → 65.8 % (2045); 65+ rises 6.2 % → 14.6 % (slide 4). Population 324 m, 6th in the world.
- **B4.** The ten megatrends hold as stated (slide 3) — notably world trade growing 3.4 %/yr and an open global economy for the "global expansion" phase of 2035-39.
- **B5.** Downstreaming of natural resources (hilirisasi) plus R&D and labour productivity is the phase-1 engine (slide 14).
- **B6.** The manufacturing share can be lifted from 18.7 % to 28 % after fifteen years of decline.
- **B7.** Social-cultural capital (gotong royong, tolerance, consensus) is a durable asset (slide 4).
- **B8.** Regions will align their long-term and medium-term plans to the national one, and the RPJPN → RPJMN → RKP cascade will hold the course (slides 19-21). Mission 8 "Kesinambungan Pembangunan" (continuity) makes continuity an explicit objective rather than an unstated assumption.
- **B9.** The governance agenda (single salary system, zero corruption, party integrity) can be delivered by the state it is meant to reform.

Implied:

- **B10.** No large external shock in 2025-2045; the projection starts from a projected 2025 baseline and runs in a straight line.
- **B11.** Indonesia keeps access to markets, finance and technology from all sides despite the "escalating competition between states" it lists as a megatrend.
- **B12.** The 2025 baseline values will be reached; they are projections, not outturns (the annex says so in a table footnote).
- **B13.** Fiscal space is sufficient; the keynote has no financing slide.

## 3. Rancangan Akhir RPJPN 2025-2045 — annex to the draft law (Bappenas)

### Publication date and time period

- **Date:** **undated**. Best guess: **second half of 2023, possibly into early 2024**. Evidence: it is the *final* draft, so it post-dates the initial draft presented in May 2023; source lines say "diolah oleh Bappenas (2023)" and "Pareto Economics (2023)"; the potential-growth chart runs to 2024 as a projection; agricultural productivity carries a 2023 value (likely an early estimate); all other data stop at 2022. It pre-dates the DPR text, because the enacted law (UU 59/2024, promulgated 13 September 2024) changed the vision wording. Record the date on the page as "final draft, c. late 2023; data to 2022/early 2023".
- **Period in focus:** **2025-2045**, four phases (Gambar 3.6.1). Historical reflection on 2005-2025 (Bab I). Several longer horizons: climate warming "beyond 1.5 °C by 2100" (Bab II opener), carrying-capacity projections to 2045 (Tabel 2.3.1), net zero emissions 2060 (Grafik 4.5.1), E7/G7 shares to 2050 (Grafik 2.1.2).
- **Standing at publication:** draft annex to a bill. **Status today:** the vision is in force as law (UU 59/2024), but this text is the draft, not the enacted annex; known difference: the law reads "NKRI yang **Bersatu**, Berdaulat, Maju, dan Berkelanjutan". Indicator values may also differ. Watermarked "Tidak untuk Dikutip".

### Normative future

Vision wording: **"Negara Kesatuan Republik Indonesia yang Berdaulat, Maju, dan Berkelanjutan"** (Gambar 3.3.1). "Negara Nusantara" from the keynote becomes "NKRI" with a nusantara description. *Maju* is defined as the **fifth-largest economy in the world**, knowledge- and innovation-based (Bab III opener). Delivery structure unchanged: 5 targets, 8 missions, 17 directions, 45 indicators, 4 phases, plus **20 game changers** (Gambar 3.7.1).

Five targets, final-draft values (Gambar 3.4.1):

| Target | 2025 (projected) | 2045 | Changed from keynote? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GNI per capita (USD) | 5,500-5,520 | 30,300 | baseline range |
| Maritime GDP share | 8.1 % | 15.0 % | target down from 17.5 |
| Manufacturing GDP share | 20.8 % | 28.0 % | baseline up from 18.7 |
| Poverty | 7.0-8.0 % | 0.5-0.8 % | baseline up |
| Gini | 0.379-0.382 | 0.290-0.320 | — |
| KTI share of GRDP | 21.4 % | 28.5 % | target up from 26.0 |
| Global Power Index | 34 (2023) | top 15 | — |
| Human Capital Index | 0.56 | 0.73 | baseline up from 0.54 |
| GHG intensity reduction | 38.6 % | 93.5 % | — |

The end state is spelt out in 45 indicators (Tabel 4.1.1-4.5.1). Among the most demanding: PISA reading 396 → 485; mean schooling 9.33 → 12 years; tertiary GER 33.94 → 60 %; R&D 0.30 → 2.2-2.3 % of GDP; **renewables 20 → 70 % of primary energy**; exports 21 → 40 % of GDP; logistics cost 13.5 → 8 % of GDP; tax ratio 10.7-11.2 → 18-20 %; bank assets 66.9 → 200 % of GDP; pension assets 8 → 60 %; employment social-security coverage 43 → 99.5 %; Asia Power Index military capability 16 → 45. The green-economy pathway takes annual emissions from 1.50 GtCO2e (2025) to 0.55 (2045) against a 2.91 baseline, with net zero in 2060.

Phased economic path (Gambar 4.2.1): growth 5.6-6.1 % (2025-29) → 6.9-7.8 % (2030-34) → 6.4-7.6 % (2035-39) → 5.4-6.7 % (2040-45); manufacturing 21.9 → 26.6 → 30.0 → 28.0 % of GDP; middle class 38 → 50 → 61 → 80 % of population.

Stance: **normative, quantified, enacted** (as law). Two growth scenarios lead to the same end state; no "without action" future is drawn, though Bab I's falling potential growth (~3.3 % by 2024) functions as the implicit warning.

### Key issues the annex focuses on

Bab I names fourteen challenges; the quantified ones are:

1. **Low and falling productivity.** TFP growth -0.66 over 2005-2019 vs +1.60 China, +1.61 Korea; Indonesia the only one of six Asian comparators whose TFP index fell 2010-19 (Grafik 1.2.1-1.2.2).
2. **Falling potential growth**, from ~5.7 % (2010) to ~3.3 % (2024) (Grafik 1.2.3).
3. **Premature deindustrialisation.** Manufacturing 27.4 % → 18.3 % of GDP, 2005-2022 (Grafik 1.1.2, 1.2.4).
4. **Weak R&D and innovation.** 0.28 % of GDP (2020); 388 researchers per million; 1,445 patents.
5. **Human-capital quality.** PISA 371-396 vs OECD ~488; stunting 21.6 %; schooling 9.3 years.
6. **Hyper-regulation.** 18,045 ministerial and 18,661 regional regulations (Grafik 1.2.7); legal development index slipped 2019-2020.
7. **Domestic integration and the Java gap.** Java 57.8 % of GDP; high logistics costs; 20 lower-middle-income provinces.
8. **Agricultural stagnation and food-system fragility**: productivity flat since 2019, negative in 2020 and 2023.
9. **Digital gap:** 62.1 % internet users (2021), mostly unproductive use.
10. **Environmental carrying capacity.** Java-Bali-NT heading for absolute water scarcity; Kalimantan losing up to 22 m ha of land cover; 34 Javanese coastal districts highly vulnerable (Tabel 2.3.1). Emissions ~1,050 MtCO2e, 56 % energy.
11. **Closing demographic window.** Dependency ratio 44.3 (2020) → 48.3 (2035) → 54.1 (2050) — the trough is now, not 2028-31 (Grafik 2.2.1).
12. **Governance, legal certainty, democratisation** "not yet optimal"; corruption persistent.

### Key assumptions behind the vision

These are the F-register already drafted in `claude/indonesia-2045-review-scope.md`, condensed:

Stated:

- **C1.** 6-7 % average growth for two decades; MIT exit 2041 (6 %) or 2038 (7 %) (Grafik 4.2.1).
- **C2.** The demographic bonus lasts roughly 15 more years and is the main engine if productivity rises (Bab II).
- **C3.** TFP can be turned positive; productivity is the binding constraint and also the lever.
- **C4.** World trade grows 3.4 %/yr; developing economies reach 71 % of output; E7 overtake G7 in the 2030s (Gambar 2.1.1, Grafik 2.1.2).
- **C5.** The global middle class reaches 8.8 bn; Indonesia's consuming class grows from 38 % to 80 % of population.
- **C6.** Technology replaces ~40 % of current jobs and the disruption is manageable through skills, a digital superplatform and a digital-transformation regulation (Gambar 4.2.4).
- **C7.** Climate change raises temperature and disaster losses but within a range the resilience measures absorb; the green-economy GHG path is attainable; nuclear power is commissioned in 2030-34 and renewables reach 70 % of primary energy (Gambar 4.2.3, Tabel 4.2.2).
- **C8.** Fiscal space is built: tax ratio nearly doubles, adaptive fiscal rules, subsidy reform, non-APBN financing (game changers 16-17; Bab VI).
- **C9.** Continuity is engineered through the RPJPN → RPJMN → RKP cascade and Mission 8; regional plans must conform (Gambar 6.1.1-6.1.3).
- **C10.** Industrial policy works spatially: Java hosts medium-high-tech, outer corridors do resource-based downstreaming, with net-export status reached sector by sector by 2035-45 (Tabel 4.2.1, Gambar 4.2.2).
- **C11.** The 2025 baselines are projections (table footnote) — the scorecard starts from a target, not an outturn.

Implied:

- **C12.** No large external shock; the projection runs in a straight line from 2023.
- **C13.** Indonesia keeps access to markets, finance and technology from all sides despite the geopolitical competition it describes; nickel and other downstreaming export markets stay open.
- **C14.** The state can simplify regulation and raise institutional capacity enough to deliver (hyper-regulation stated as a problem, assumed solvable: single regulatory agency, single prosecution system).
- **C15.** Financial deepening on the scale assumed (bank assets 200 % of GDP, credit 80-90 %) is compatible with the macro-stability target.

### Observations for the review

1. **COVID-19 is the hinge.** The 2017 path required 5.5 % for 2015-20 and 6.5 % for 2020-25; 2020 was about -2.1 %. The 2023 documents do not score 2017 — they restart from a projected 2025 baseline. For Norantz the 2017 deck is a "superseded vision" case: its claims and enabling assumptions can be tested (demography, deindustrialisation, R&D, inequality), its targets shown with last-known status and marked no longer in force.
2. **The ambition was recalibrated, not abandoned.** High-income moved from 2035 to 2038-41; GDP rank from 4 to 5; industry from 32 % to 28 %; investment share dropped sharply while the productivity story took its place. Poverty was relaxed, inequality and KTI tightened.
3. **The demographic argument weakened between vintages.** 2017 placed the dependency trough in 2028-31; the 2023 annex has it already passed (44.3 in 2020) and rising to 54.1 by 2050. The "bonus" now has to be captured in the plan's first half.
4. **New topics in 2023 absent in 2017:** IKN; nickel downstreaming as a proven model; a national digital superplatform; CBDC and multi-currency finance; space; single prosecution system; 13-year compulsory schooling; Regsosek; the FEW nexus; net zero 2060; nuclear with a date (2030-34). **Absent in all three:** generative AI as a distinct force; tariff or trade-war scenarios; a change of government or policy discontinuity; a post-COVID pandemic; critical-mineral export restrictions by others. These feed the absent-topic check.
5. **Framing shifted from "reform" to "transformation".** The 2017 deck is pillars plus sectoral targets; 2023 is 8/17/45 numerology, game changers, SDG continuity and an explicit continuity mission. The keynote's "Re-Form → Trans-Form" slide is the authors' own verdict on the 2005-2025 approach.
6. **Three vintages, four texts.** Initial draft (keynote), final draft (annex), enacted annex (not in hand) and the 2017 deck. Figures moved between the first two (maritime 17.5 → 15.0; KTI 26.0 → 28.5; HCI baseline 0.54 → 0.56; manufacturing baseline 18.7 → 20.8). Expect further movement in the enacted annex. Every figure on a page needs its vintage label.

### Limits

- Both 2023 documents carry a "Tidak untuk Dikutip" watermark; the 2017 deck does not.
- The annex date is inferred, not printed.
- Values marked "~" in the extractions were read from charts and should not be rated.
- The 2025 baselines in the annex are projections; the keynote's "2025" column mixes 2022 outturns and projections.
- The 2017 deck has internal inconsistencies to settle before testing: industry growth 7.8 % (slide 9) vs 8.1 % (slide 26); dependency ratio 2045 of 50.2 % (card) vs 50.1 % (chart).
