# Template test: España 2050

Version 0.1, 16 November 2026. Findings from one test fill.

The report page template was filled with the España 2050 pilot to see where it breaks. Of 17 sections, 2 filled cleanly, 7 filled with strain, 4 filled in part and 4 broke or stayed empty. The filled page was `espana-2050-page-draft.md` version 0.1 (git history, commit `0fd0026`); that file is now draft 0.2, rebuilt to spec v0.2 and brand v0.1.

The test used only what the pilot already held: the assumption register doc (clusters 1 to 4) and the fiscal and housing evidence review (clusters 5 and 6). Nothing was looked up again. Where the page needed a judgement the pilot never made, a trial one was written and tagged.

## The breaks that matter

### 1. The same evidence gets opposite ratings

Migration at three times the assumed rate is rated "invalidated" for the population baseline (F5) and "strengthened" for the report's migration sensitivity test (7.5). Household growth at three times the assumed rate is rated "strengthened" (6.6); population above its projection is rated "invalidated" (6.3); growth above the baseline range is rated "weakened" (1.3).

Each rating can be defended against its own item. Side by side on a public page they read as contradictions. "Strengthened" is the weak point: it can mean "the report looks more right" or "the variable went further than the report said".

Six of the pilot's ratings also carry qualifiers the scale does not have: "invalidated on level, confirmed on mechanism", "confirmed, provisionally", "invalidated to date", "strengthened as risks", and two split ratings.

This is method decision 1 showing up on the page. It should be closed before any page is published.

### 2. One rating per theme hides the spread

The population theme shows "invalidated". Its eight items are 2 invalidated, 4 weakened, 1 strengthened and 1 confirmed. The fiscal theme shows "weakened" over 2 confirmed, 2 strengthened, 4 weakened and 1 mixed. The template had no rule for which item speaks for the theme. The test used the item most chapters depend on and added a spread line.

### 3. The tally and the coverage figure have no agreed unit

Counted by table row, as the pilot did, the tally is 8 confirmed, 7 strengthened, 13 weakened, 5 invalidated. Counted by register item it is 11, 7, 14 and 5. Coverage is "6 of 9 themes" or "38 of 102 items", which give very different impressions. The register also holds facts, projections and open questions, so "assumptions reviewed" is the wrong label.

A reader or journalist will quote "5 invalidated, 14 weakened" whatever the page says about not grading.

### 4. The drivers section does not fit this kind of report

The template defines a driver as a force that separates one scenario from another. In España 2050, policy separates them. The outside forces are the same in both scenarios, so they are assumptions and already sit in section 7. Section 6 ended up as a list of policy levers that repeats section 7.

The definition suits a set of exploratory scenarios. It does not suit a baseline set against a goal.

### 5. The report is not one of the three types

España 2050 has quantified scenarios and is also a vision: one scenario is a projection, the other a goal with a roadmap. Three things follow:

- The fixed text "scenarios are not predictions" is inaccurate here.
- Equal scenario cards make a warning and a goal look like two forecasts.
- "What it needs to happen" works for the goal and is odd for the baseline.

### 6. "Still plausible by 2050" fits only some assumptions

The second time view worked for two of six themes. It failed in three ways:

- **Already passed.** The 2050 population level was reached in 2025. None of the three options says that.
- **Standing conditions.** "A calm external environment" has no 2050 value to be plausible or not.
- **Projection against projection.** For pensions, the evidence is a newer 2050 projection, so the two views are the same test.

### 7. The "revisit" rule gives a wrong answer

The proposed rule marks a recommendation "revisit" when an assumption it rests on is weakened or invalidated. The growth theme is weakened, yet the pilot reads the productivity and education proposals as still standing and more urgent. A failed assumption can strengthen the case for a recommendation.

The rule also needs a list of recommendations. The pilot never logged the report's 200 proposals, so nothing can be tied to register items.

### 8. Several sections need judgements no stage makes

Statuses for the nine questions, the choice of three headline findings, the list of major events and the trigger levels were all written fresh for this test. None has a rubric or a human checkpoint. The question scale also lacks "not yet reviewed" and "more pressing".

## Smaller breaks

- **Hindsight.** The word search flags Ukraine as absent from a report published nine months before the invasion. The omissions section needs to separate known risks left out from events nobody could have dated.
- **Targets.** Fifty rows will not fit a page. A rule is needed for which to show.
- **Themes drift.** The fiscal pass tested an item from the political delivery theme. Two "not yet reviewed" themes already have evidence gathered elsewhere.
- **One "as of" date is misleading.** The page says 16 November 2026. The figures run from 2022 (home ownership by age) to October 2026.
- **Afterlife is also an assumption.** The report assumes a cross-party agreement follows. Section 13 says "no rating", but that item will be rated.
- **Limits.** The fiscal and housing pass lists more than 20. They belong on the figures they affect, not in one paragraph.

## A finding about sources

The source rule keeps media out of the evidence. In the fiscal and housing pass, several official figures were reached through news pages reporting them, and those pages appear in its citation list. The limits log calls these "secondary reports" without saying they are media. The rule needs a line on media as a carrier of official figures, and those figures need checking at the primary source before publication.

## What worked

- **Section 8.** Reading element by element gave a clear, fair answer to "which future are we in?" with no single verdict.
- **Ranking by dependency.** The reader meets the assumption that feeds seven chapters first.
- **The word search for absent topics.** It is concrete, repeatable and easy to explain.
- **The stated or implied label.** It did its job on the external environment theme.
- **The 120-word summary.** Four sentences in the set order were enough.
- **Gaps confirmed.** Every gap the spec predicted showed up, and no section was filled from outside the pilot.

## Proposed changes

Each change is a proposal. None has been made.

| # | Change | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rate each item on two separate questions: did the variable move as the report said, and does the report's argument still hold? Allow a short qualifier. | Method, decision 1 |
| 2 | Show each theme's lead item with its rating, plus a spread line. Define the lead item as the one most chapters depend on. | Template section 7 |
| 3 | Count the tally and coverage by register item, and show both "themes" and "items". Rename "assumptions reviewed" to "items reviewed". | Template sections 0 and 1 |
| 4 | Replace the single report type with two fields: form (numbers or stories) and purpose (exploring or goal-setting). Write a "how to read" text for each purpose. | Method stage 1; template sections 0, 2, 5 |
| 5 | Split section 6 in two: forces the report took as given, and levers that separate the scenarios. Drop whichever is empty. | Template section 6; spec |
| 6 | Apply "by the horizon year" only to items with a horizon value. Add "already passed". | Method, decision 2; template section 7 |
| 7 | Log recommendations at stage 2. Change the rule to ask whether the evidence weakens or strengthens the case, and add "more urgent". | Method, decision 5; template section 11 |
| 8 | Add "not yet reviewed" and "more pressing" to the question scale. Raise the limit to the report's own number of questions. | Template section 4 |
| 9 | Give "closer to" a direction: above both, below both, between. | Template section 8 |
| 10 | Add a rule for headline targets, a five-value position scale and a note column. | Template section 9 |
| 11 | Split omissions into "known at the time and left out" and "arose after publication". | Template section 10 |
| 12 | Show a data date per figure. Keep "reviewed on" for the page. | Template sections 0 and 7 |
| 13 | Add a "not yet invited" state for the authors' response. | Template section 15 |
| 14 | State how media pages that carry official figures are treated. | Method, source tiers |

Changes 1, 3 and 6 alter ratings and counts already in the pilot.

## Limits of this test

- One report, and an easy one: numbers, dated targets and strong official data. A narrative report will break different sections, probably 8, 9 and 12.
- The trial judgements are mine and unreviewed. They show what the template asks for, not what the answers should be.
- The page was read as text. Nobody has seen it laid out or on a phone, and no reader has tested it.
- The fiscal and housing review was read from an earlier chat, not from a saved doc. Its findings are not yet in the register.
