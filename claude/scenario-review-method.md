# Norantz scenario review method

Version 0.2, 16 November 2026. Draft for review.

This document describes how Norantz reviews a past national scenario or vision report against current developments.

**What changed from version 0.1**

- Reports are now classified at intake by stance, quantification and standing, and routed to one of two tracks: a normative track for visions and an exploratory track for scenario sets.
- The register now separates claims about the world from goals and from commitments. Each has its own status scale.
- The normative track has a new stage, the change scan. It records the emerging issues, trends and major shifts since publication and maps each one to the assumptions it bears on, as a challenge or an opportunity.
- A standing check at intake establishes whether a vision is still in force before the full review is run.
- Open decision 8 (report types) now has a worked proposal. Six new decisions are added.

Version 0.1 was drawn from one pilot, España 2050. The two tracks in this version were prompted by the review of `ASEAN National Foresight Scenario Reports.docx`, a secondary synthesis that describes most ASEAN national reports as normative visions. Neither track has been run on a second report. See "Known limits".

## Purpose

For each report, the method answers five questions. The second and fifth differ by track.

| # | Question | Normative track | Exploratory track |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Which assumptions have held? | Same in both tracks. | Same in both tracks. |
| 2 | Where do things stand against the report's futures? | Is the country on the path the vision requires, and is the path still reachable? | Which scenario elements does the evidence point to? |
| 3 | Are dated targets ahead of, on or behind their path? | Core of the review. | Usually not applicable. |
| 4 | Which recommendations still stand? | Recommendations and commitments, including whether commitments were delivered. | Implications, where the report gives any. |
| 5 | What has changed around the report? | Which emerging issues, trends and shifts open new challenges or opportunities for the vision, and which assumptions do they bear on? | Did what happened fall inside the range the scenarios spanned? |

## Classifying a report (stage 1)

Three attributes are recorded at intake. The first decides the track. The other two decide which tests within the track can be run.

**Stance**

| Stance | Test | Track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normative | The report describes one desired future and a route to it. | Normative |
| Exploratory | The report sets out more than one possible future and does not prefer one. | Exploratory |
| Hybrid | The report sets out more than one future but argues for one of them. España 2050 is this type: a baseline and a convergence path, with the second as the goal. | Normative, with the non-preferred futures used as comparison paths |

**Quantification**

| Level | Meaning | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantified | Dated numeric targets or modelled paths. | Target scorecard and required-rate test can be run. |
| Partly quantified | Some numeric targets, mostly narrative. | Scorecard for the numeric part; proxies or signposts for the rest. |
| Qualitative | Aspirations or stories without numbers. | Proxies (normative) or signposts (exploratory), each labelled as the reviewer's. |

**Standing**

Recorded in two parts: what force the report had when published (law, executive order, adopted policy without legal force, advisory, non-government), and its status today (in force, revised, superseded, lapsed).

Standing decides how much of the commitment record can be checked. A vision tied to budgets by law leaves a trail that an advisory report does not. Status today decides whether the full review is run at all; see the standing check below.

How the report was made (expert panel, public consultation, joint work with an outside body) is recorded as context for the public page. It does not change the track.

Classification is made from the report's own text, not from secondary descriptions. A human confirms it.

## The register (both tracks)

Version 0.1 logged facts, trends and assumptions together. Version 0.2 adds a class to each entry, because a missed goal is not a failed assumption and should not be rated as one.

| Class | What it is | Status scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim | A statement about the world: a fact, a trend, a projection, or a cause-and-effect assumption. | Confirmed, Strengthened, Weakened, Invalidated, No evidence (unchanged from 0.1) |
| Goal | A desired end state or a dated target. | Met, Ahead, On path, Behind, No longer in force, Not measurable |
| Commitment | A measure, reform, institution or spending line the report says will be put in place. | Delivered, Partly delivered, Not delivered, Dropped, Not assessed |
| Signpost | An observable indicator that a scenario is unfolding. Exploratory track only. | Observed, Partly observed, Not observed, Too early |

**Register entry**

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| ID | Framing assumptions are numbered F1, F2 and so on. Chapter items use chapter and item number, such as 5.4. |
| Class | Claim, Goal, Commitment or Signpost. |
| Item | One sentence, in the report's terms. |
| Status | Stated (the report says it outright) or Implied (the argument needs it but does not say it). |
| Pages | Page references in the source. |
| Depends on | IDs of other items this one rests on. |
| What would challenge it | The kind of evidence that would weaken or contradict the item. Written before any evidence is gathered. |

**Evidence table row**

Item ID | Report said (with year) | Evidence now (figures and dates) | Source | Rating or status

**Source tiers**

- Tier 1: official statistics and institutional reports (national statistics office, central bank, fiscal authority, Eurostat or equivalent).
- Tier 2: think-tank and research analysis.
- Tier 3: media. Not used as evidence for ratings.

Every finding carries an "as of" date. Every figure taken from a secondary account of a primary source is marked as such in the limits log.

## Normative track

For visions and hybrids. The logic of a vision is: here is where we want to be, here is what we will do, and here is what the world must allow. The review tests each of the three, then asks what has changed around them.

| Stage | What happens | Output | Human checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N1. Intake and standing check | Record the profile and classification. Record the desired end state in the report's words, the milestones, and the "without action" case if the report gives one. Establish whether the vision is in force, revised, superseded or lapsed. | Report profile, standing note | Confirm classification and standing |
| N2. Register | Log claims, goals and commitments. Run the vision assumption checklist and the absent-topic check. Record dependencies. For qualitative goals, propose proxy indicators. | Register | Validate implied assumptions and proxies |
| N3. Consistency check | Flag places where the report contradicts its own figures. | Inconsistency list | Decide which figure to test against |
| N4. Cluster and rank | Group items into clusters. Rank assumptions by how many goals rest on each and how directly each can be tested. | Ranked cluster list | Choose clusters and order |
| N5. Evidence pass | Gather current evidence for claims, goals and commitments from the agreed source tier. | Evidence tables | None during the pass |
| N6. Change scan | Record emerging issues, trends and major shifts since publication. Map each to the register items it bears on, as a challenge or an opportunity. | Change register, link table | Validate the list of changes and the direction of each link |
| N7. Rate | Apply the status scales. Run the required-rate test on quantified goals. | Rated register | Review ratings, especially "invalidated" and "behind" |
| N8. Interpret | Produce the path reading, the assumption findings, the pressure reading from the change scan, and a view on each group of recommendations. | Findings | Review before publication |
| N9. Limits log | List secondary sources, sources that could not be opened, unverified figures, and every reviewer-made proxy. | Limits section | None |

### Standing check (N1)

Visions are exposed to changes of government. The secondary synthesis reports, for example, that Malaysia's TN50 was replaced within two years of launch. Testing dated targets that no government is pursuing tells the reader little.

| Status today | What is reviewed |
| --- | --- |
| In force | Full review. |
| Revised | Full review against the original. Revised targets are shown beside the originals. |
| Superseded or lapsed | Reduced review. Claims and enabling assumptions are tested, because they often carry into the successor. Goals are shown with their last known status and marked "no longer in force". Commitments are not assessed. The successor document is named and linked. |

The standing check may need media and secondary sources. It carries no rating, so this does not conflict with keeping media out of ratings.

### Vision assumption checklist (N2)

Visions are written to build support, so the conditions for failure are often left unstated. The pilot's largest finding of this kind was an unstated calm external environment. Stage N2 checks each report for the assumptions below. Each one found is logged as a claim, with page references if stated, or labelled implied if the argument needs it but does not say it.

| Type | The question to ask of the report |
| --- | --- |
| Enabling conditions | What must the outside world provide? World growth, open trade, access to finance, stable neighbours, a climate within certain bounds. |
| Growth path | What rate of growth, sustained for how long, does the end state need? |
| Causal links | For each main goal: which measures are meant to produce it, and by what mechanism? |
| State capacity | Does the plan assume the state can fund, staff and carry out the measures? |
| Continuity | Does the plan assume the vision stays in force across changes of government? |
| Baselines | Which population, labour force, resource or technology projections does the plan take as given? |
| No large shock | Does the path allow for interruption, or does it run in a straight line? |

### Proxies for qualitative goals (N2)

A goal such as "a comfortable and secure life for all" cannot be tested as written. The reviewer proposes one to three proxy indicators per goal. Indicators the government itself adopted in later plans are preferred, since they reflect the authors' own reading of the goal. Every proxy is labelled as the reviewer's, validated by a human, and listed in the limits log. A goal with no defensible proxy is marked "not measurable".

### Change scan (N6)

**Purpose.** The evidence pass looks back: has each assumption held so far? The change scan looks at what is new in the vision's operating environment since publication and asks what it means for the vision, and above all for the assumptions underneath it. It answers question 5.

"Environment" here means the whole operating environment (economic, technological, political, social and ecological), not only the natural environment.

**Three kinds of change**

| Kind | Definition | Evidence bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major shift | A discontinuity that has already happened and changed operating conditions. It has a date. | Tier 1 |
| Trend | A direction of change that is established and measurable over several years, and that began, sped up, slowed or reversed since publication. | Tier 1, or Tier 2 with data |
| Emerging issue | An early development whose scale and direction are still uncertain. | Tier 2 allowed. Labelled as emerging. |

**Step 1. Collect candidates.** Candidates come from a fixed set of sources, not from open-ended generation, so that two runs on the same report produce similar lists:

- the country's own later plans, reviews and progress reports, which say what the government itself thinks has changed;
- later foresight and risk publications from recognised regional and international bodies, held in a shared source pack;
- the absent-topic check from stage N2;
- developments met during the evidence pass that did not fit any register item.

The scan covers a fixed list of domains: demographic and social; economic and trade; technological; environmental and climate; domestic political and institutional; geopolitical and security; fiscal and financial; international rules and regulation. Each domain returns at least one candidate or an explicit "none found".

**Step 2. Screen.** A candidate is kept if it bears on at least one register item. A candidate that bears on no register item but plainly matters for the country is kept and marked "outside the vision's frame". Everything else is logged and set aside.

**Step 3. Map to the register.** Each kept change is linked to the items it bears on, starting with the framing assumptions and the highest-ranked clusters. Each link records:

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Direction | Challenge, Opportunity, Both, Unclear |
| Acts on | The assumption (is it still true?), the path (is a goal easier or harder, cheaper or dearer, faster or slower to reach?), the means (does the chosen instrument still work?), or the goal (is it still the right goal?) |
| Mechanism | One sentence on how the change bears on the item. A link with no stated mechanism is not recorded. |
| Timing | Already acting, Expected within the horizon, Uncertain |
| Confidence | Established, Emerging, Speculative |

Direction is judged against the vision's own goals, not against the reviewer's view of what is good. A change is an opportunity if it makes a goal of the vision easier to reach, and a challenge if it makes one harder. This keeps the scan descriptive.

**Step 4. Read the pressure on each assumption.** For each ranked assumption, the links are read together:

| Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Under pressure | One or more established changes challenge it. |
| Supported | One or more established changes work in its favour. |
| Mixed | Established changes pull both ways. |
| Watch | Only emerging or speculative links so far. |
| Untouched | No links found. |

There is no composite score. The strongest link is named in each case.

**Step 5. Find the gaps.** Two lists come out of the link table:

- challenges for which the vision contains no commitment;
- opportunities the vision's commitments do not use.

Both are stated as facts about the text ("the vision contains no measure addressing"), not as criticism.

**How the scan feeds the rest of the review**

- It does not change a "tracking to date" rating. That rests on the evidence pass alone.
- It informs the "still plausible by the horizon year" half of the rating (decision 2).
- It adds a second route to "revisit" for recommendations: a recommendation is marked "revisit: new conditions" when a load-bearing assumption is under pressure from an established change, even if the evidence to date has not yet weakened it.
- Emerging issues with a "watch" reading supply indicators for the "what to watch next" section of the public page.

**Change register entry**

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| ID | X1, X2 and so on. |
| Change | One sentence. |
| Kind | Major shift, Trend, or Emerging issue. |
| Domain | From the fixed list. |
| Since | Date or year it began or became visible. |
| Evidence | Figures, dates, source and tier. |
| In the report? | Addressed, Mentioned in passing, or Absent. Recorded as a fact. It is not a judgement on whether the authors should have foreseen it. |
| Links | Register IDs, each with the five link fields above. |

**An illustration of the format, not a finding.** A vision published in 2016 that relies on labour-intensive export manufacturing would have three kinds of entry. A change in a major partner's tariff policy is a major shift, acting on an enabling condition, as a challenge. The spread of generative AI since late 2022 is a trend, acting on the causal link between low-cost labour and export growth as a challenge, and on a productivity goal as an opportunity, so "both". An early proposal for a regional carbon border charge is an emerging issue, acting on the path to an export goal, with timing uncertain.

**Relation to the absent-topic check.** The absent-topic check reads the report and asks what is missing. The change scan reads the world and asks what is new. A new development that the report does not mention appears in both.

### Required-rate test (N7)

For each quantified goal, four figures are computed:

1. the rate of change the goal needed from the publication year;
2. the rate achieved since;
3. the rate now needed from the latest figure to reach the goal on time;
4. the best rate the country has sustained over a comparable period on record.

The finding is stated plainly, for example: "reaching the target now needs a rate above the best ten-year rate on record". This gives the "reachable" half of the rating a mechanical basis. Where a gap follows a dated shock recorded in the change scan, the link is noted beside the figure.

### Path reading (N8)

Question 2 for a vision is answered goal by goal: on the path, behind it, or ahead of it, and still reachable or not on the required-rate test. For hybrids, the non-preferred futures are shown as comparison paths, as the pilot did with España 2050's baseline. If the country is on none of the report's paths, decision 4 applies.

## Exploratory track

For scenario sets that do not prefer one future. Such reports usually say they are not forecasts. The review does not ask whether a scenario "came true". It asks whether the report's drivers mattered, which elements can be seen, and whether events stayed inside the range the scenarios spanned.

This track is a first draft. It has not been run on any report.

| Stage | What happens | Output | Human checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1. Intake and availability check | Record the profile and classification. Record the scenario logic (axes or drivers), the stated purpose, and whether a full public text exists. | Report profile | Confirm classification. Stop if there is no public text. |
| E2. Register | Log claims about drivers, separating those the report treats as near-certain from those it treats as uncertain. Log signposts for each scenario, using the report's own where it gives them. Run the absent-topic check. | Register | Validate reviewer-derived signposts |
| E3. Consistency check | As in the normative track. | Inconsistency list | Decide which version to test |
| E4. Cluster and rank | Rank drivers by how many scenarios turn on each. | Ranked driver list | Choose drivers and order |
| E5. Evidence pass | Gather evidence for driver claims and signposts. | Evidence tables | None during the pass |
| E6. Tests | Run the four tests below. | Rated register, coverage list | Review the coverage list |
| E7. Interpret | Read the results element by element. No scenario is named as "the" outcome unless most elements agree. | Findings | Review before publication |
| E8. Limits log | As in the normative track, plus every reviewer-derived signpost. | Limits section | None |

**The four tests (E6)**

| Test | Question | Scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driver test | Did the forces the report named turn out to matter? Did those treated as near-certain behave as described? | Claim scale |
| Signpost test | For each scenario, which signposts can be observed? | Signpost scale, reported per scenario and per element |
| Coverage test | Did major developments since publication fall inside the range the scenario set spanned? | Inside the set, At the edge, Outside the set |
| Implications test | Where the report gives implications or advice, do they hold across what happened? | Still stands, Revisit, Not assessed |

The coverage test uses the same collection and screening steps as the change scan, so both tracks draw on one change register format. The difference is in the reading: for a vision, each change is a challenge or an opportunity; for a scenario set, each change is inside or outside the range.

There is no target scorecard and no "behind". If the report contains no recommendations, question 4 is marked "not applicable".

**Availability check.** Some exploratory work is made for use inside government and only partly published. Singapore's national scenarios appear to be an example: searches found published foresight volumes and driving-force summaries but not the scenario sets themselves. This has not been confirmed with the publisher. A report with no full public text is not reviewed. Its published summaries may be reviewed as a separate, smaller item.

## What the pilot showed

The España 2050 review produced a register of 102 items, including ten framing assumptions, grouped into nine clusters. Six clusters were tested: demography and migration, continuity and external environment, growth mechanism, technology, fiscal envelope, and housing. Three were not: climate timing, the EU-8 benchmark, and political delivery.

Lessons that carry over to other reports:

- **A few assumptions carry most of the report.** One demographic projection fed seven of nine chapters. Ranking clusters by dependency found this before any evidence was gathered.
- **Omissions are assumptions.** The benign external environment (F10) was never stated. It was found by checking which topics the report did not mention.
- **Knock-on effects reopen other chapters.** Once the population baseline failed, housing, education and territorial items that depended on it needed retesting. The register now has a "depends on" field.
- **The evidence may point outside the report's scenarios.** Spain was on neither of the two paths. The method needs a defined way to say so.
- **Internal inconsistencies are common.** Five were found. They must be settled before testing, or the same item can be rated two ways.

**How España 2050 sits in version 0.2.** It is a hybrid, quantified, advisory report, so it runs on the normative track with its baseline as a comparison path. The pilot transfers well. Three things are missing from it under this version: the register has no class field, the "political delivery" cluster (its commitments) was not tested, and no change scan was run. F10 is the kind of finding the change scan is designed to produce on purpose.

## What was specific to España 2050

These features made the pilot easier than a typical report and should not be assumed elsewhere:

- **Quantified scenarios.** A baseline and a convergence path, each with numbers, allowed direct comparison with outturn data.
- **Dated targets.** The 2030 targets used published European indicators, so a scorecard was straightforward.
- **Strong official data.** INE, Banco de España, AIReF and Eurostat publish frequently and openly.
- **A single external benchmark.** Targets were defined against eight EU countries, which gave a fixed comparison group.

## Open design decisions

Each decision lists a proposed default. These are proposals for discussion, not agreed positions.

| # | Decision | Why it matters | Proposed default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rating thresholds | The pilot set no numeric line between "weakened" and "invalidated". The same applies to "on path" and "behind". | Define thresholds per item type before the evidence pass, and record them in the register. |
| 2 | Horizon | Only five years of a thirty-year horizon were testable. A projection off track today can still prove right. | Split the rating in two: tracking to date (from the evidence pass), and still plausible by the horizon year (informed by the required-rate test and the change scan). |
| 3 | Implied assumptions | These are the reviewer's reading, not the report's words. They are also where the largest findings came from. | Publish them, labelled as implied, after human validation. |
| 4 | Missing scenarios | Naming a path the report did not model is an editorial act. | Describe the observed path in neutral terms and state that the report did not model it. |
| 5 | Recommendation relevance | This is the most subjective step. | Tie each recommendation to the register items it rests on. "Revisit" only when a load-bearing item is weakened, invalidated, or under pressure from an established change. |
| 6 | Coverage before publishing | The pilot tested six of nine clusters. | Publish with untested clusters shown as "not yet reviewed". |
| 7 | Verification | Pilot figures came mostly from extracts of source pages, and some from secondary accounts. | Spot-check every figure behind an "invalidated" or "behind" status against the primary source. Use an independent check for headline findings. |
| 8 | Report type handling | Different report types need different tests. | **Proposal now written:** three intake attributes and two tracks, as set out above. Needs agreement. |
| 9 | Country source packs | Source quality and openness vary by country. | Build and approve a source pack per country before the first evidence pass. Add a shared pack of foresight and risk sources for the change scan. |
| 10 | Cross-report taxonomy | Shared clusters allow comparison across countries but force reports into one mould. | A shared top-level taxonomy, with report-specific clusters beneath it. The change scan's domain list should match it. |
| 11 | Public layer | A full register is too much for a general reader. | A short findings page per report, with the full register behind it. |
| 12 | Languages | The pilot report was in Spanish and the review in English. | Decide per report; at minimum the report's own language and English. |
| 13 | Refresh cadence | Findings date quickly. The change scan dates fastest of all. | Re-run the evidence pass and the change scan on a fixed cycle and keep dated versions. |
| 14 | Media as evidence | Deferred in the pilot. | Keep media out of ratings. Allow it for the standing check and to flag candidates for the change scan, which must then be evidenced at Tier 1 or 2. |
| 15 | Scope | Most national reports in some regions are visions or plans, not scenario sets. The project brief speaks of scenarios. | Include visions that set a long-horizon end state. Exclude ordinary medium-term plans. This version assumes that default. |
| 16 | Proxies for qualitative goals | Choosing an indicator for an aspiration is the reviewer's act and shapes the finding. | Prefer indicators the government adopted later. Label every proxy. Human validation required. |
| 17 | Superseded visions | A full review of an abandoned vision may mislead; none at all loses the test of its assumptions. | Reduced review, as in the standing check. |
| 18 | Change scan size and repeatability | An open-ended scan will differ between runs and can grow without limit. | Fixed sources and domains. Run the collection step twice independently; changes found in only one run go to human review. Publish at most twelve changes per report, with the rest in the register. |
| 19 | Public wording for goal status | "Behind" on a government's vision can read as a verdict on that government. | Report the figure and the gap first, the label second. Test softer labels such as "below the path" with readers. |
| 20 | Change scan on the exploratory track | The coverage test uses the same machinery, but challenge and opportunity have no meaning without a preferred future. | Coverage reading only. Revisit after an exploratory pilot. |

Decisions 1, 2 and 15 should be closed first. Decisions 1 and 2 change ratings already made in the pilot. Decision 15 decides how much of the corpus is in scope.

## Pilots

Version 0.1 called for a second pilot on a narrative-style report. On the evidence of the ASEAN synthesis, normative visions are the more common type, so the order is changed:

1. **Second pilot: a normative, quantified vision** with about ten years elapsed, to test the standing check, the item classes, the required-rate test and the change scan. Vietnam 2035 is a candidate, subject to classifying it from the primary text.
2. **Third pilot: a normative, qualitative vision**, to test proxies. AmBisyon Natin 2040 is a candidate.
3. **Fourth pilot: an exploratory scenario set** with a full public text, to test the exploratory track. No candidate is confirmed.

España 2050 should also get a change scan and a commitment pass in its second rating round.

## Known limits of this method document

- It is still based on one pilot. The normative track extends that pilot; the exploratory track is untested.
- The picture of ASEAN reports comes from a secondary synthesis that classifies reports by assertion and contains errors. For example, it dates Brunei's vision to 2014, though other sources date it to 2007, and it lists a think-tank series beside government reports. No ASEAN report has yet been classified from its own text.
- The change scan is the most interpretive stage after implied assumptions and recommendations. Its safeguards (fixed sources, fixed domains, a required mechanism for each link, two runs) are proposals and have not been tried.
- Time and cost per report were not measured in the pilot. The change scan adds to both.
- Human checkpoints remain informal. Who reviews, and to what standard, is not yet defined.
