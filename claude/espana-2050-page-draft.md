## 1. At a glance

**In brief**

España 2050 was published by the Spanish government's foresight office in May 2021 as the basis for a long-term national strategy. It set a future without reform against one in which Spain catches up with eight leading EU countries by 2050, and assumed slow population growth and a calm world outside. Five years on, Spain is on neither path: the economy has grown faster than both, by adding workers through migration, while productivity has not closed the gap. The proposals on productivity, education, pensions and care still stand; those on migration, tax and housing need a second look.

**Three things to know** **[STRAIN]** The pilot has at least six findings of this weight. Choosing three was my call.

1. Spain had 49.8 million residents on 1 July 2026, more than the 49.3 million the report used for 2050.
2. Productivity per hour has stayed at about 74% of the level of the eight benchmark countries for 18 years. The report's central diagnosis holds (October 2026).
3. House prices rose 12.2% in the year to the second quarter of 2026. The report assumed they would rise in line with inflation.

**Assumptions reviewed** **[STRAIN]**

11 confirmed · 7 strengthened · 14 weakened · 5 invalidated · 1 mixed · 64 not yet reviewed

This counts register items. The pilot counted table rows and reached different numbers. The items also include facts, projections and open questions, not only assumptions.

## 2. How to read this page

Scenarios are not predictions. They describe different ways the future could unfold, so that today's decisions can be tested against them. **[STRAIN]** This is not accurate here. The report's baseline is a projection of past trends, and its second scenario is a goal.

This page does not grade the report. It shows which of the report's assumptions the evidence supports so far, and which deserve a second look.

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed | Evidence matches the report. |
| Strengthened | Evidence goes further in the same direction. |
| Weakened | The direction holds, but the size or timing does not. |
| Invalidated | Evidence contradicts the report. |
| Not yet reviewed | Not yet testable, or not yet checked. |

This report gave numbers for each scenario, so we compare them with published figures.

- **The moment:** Written during the COVID-19 pandemic, which the report treats as a passing shock with recovery by 2022–2023. - **How the scenarios were built:** The 27 EU states were sorted into low, medium and high performers. Spain's past 30 to 40 years were reviewed, past trends were projected to 2050 as a baseline, and a second path was drawn in which reforms close the gap with the high group. A roadmap of 50 targets and more than 200 proposals works back from that second path.

## 5. The scenarios

The report has two scenarios. Every chapter sets a baseline without reform against a path that closes Spain's gap with eight leading EU countries by 2050.

**[STRAIN]** The two are different kinds of thing. The first is a warning built by projection. The second is a goal. Equal cards make them look like two forecasts. The stories were written for this test; the pilot holds no plain-language scenario summaries.

**Baseline**

- **The story:** Spain carries on as it did from 1980 to 2019, with no major reforms. A shrinking workforce slows the economy and strains the welfare state.
- **In 2050:** Growth of 0.3–1.1% a year. The population aged 16 to 64 is 3.7 million smaller. Pension spending reaches 15.2–16.9% of GDP.
- **What it needs to happen:** No major policy change. Net migration of about 197,000 a year. No lasting shocks.

**Convergence**

- **The story:** Significant reforms close or narrow the gap with Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands and Sweden.
- **In 2050:** Productivity is 50% higher and the employment rate is 80%, giving growth of 1.5% a year. Tax revenue is 43% of GDP. All electricity is renewable.
- **What it needs to happen:** More than 200 proposals delivered. A cross-party agreement that lasts several legislatures. Supportive EU funds and climate policy.

## 7. Key assumptions

**[STRAIN]** applies to every block: one rating per theme hides a spread, shown in the last line of each. "By 2050" is **[TRIAL]** throughout; the pilot did not produce it.

**1. Population and migration**

- **The report assumed:** 49.3 million people in 2050, with net migration averaging 197,000 a year. (Stated)
- **How much rests on it:** Used in seven of nine chapters.
- **Evidence now:** 49,801,559 residents on 1 July 2026. Net migration was 727,005 in 2022, 642,296 in 2023 and 626,268 in 2024. [INE population](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ECP2T26.htm) · [INE migration](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/EMCR2024.htm)
- **So far:** Invalidated
- **By 2050:** None of the three options fits. The 2050 level has already been passed.
- **Spread:** 8 items: 2 invalidated, 4 weakened, 1 strengthened, 1 confirmed.

**2. Continuity and a calm external environment**

- **The report assumed:** Open trade, stable energy supply, low inflation and interest rates, and no major new claims on public spending. (Implied by the report's argument)
- **How much rests on it:** All chapters.
- **Evidence now:** High US tariffs, an energy shock in 2022 and another in 2026, inflation forecast at 3.6% for 2026, and defence spending at 2% of GDP since 2025. [Banco de España](https://www.bde.es/f/webbe/SES/Secciones/Publicaciones/PublicacionesAnuales/InformesAnuales/25/InfAnual_2025_Breve.pdf) · [BBVA Research](https://www.bbvaresearch.com/publicaciones/espana-asi-esta-cambiando-el-gasto-en-defensa/)
- **So far:** Invalidated
- **By 2050:** Does not apply. This is a standing condition, not a value for 2050.
- **Spread:** 6 items: 1 invalidated, 2 weakened, 1 strengthened, 2 confirmed.

**3. How growth happens**

- **The report assumed:** Without reform, growth of 0.3–1.1% a year from 2023 as the workforce shrinks. With reform, productivity rises 50%. (Stated)
- **How much rests on it:** Five of nine chapters.
- **Evidence now:** GDP grew 2.4% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024 and 2.6% in 2025, mostly by adding workers. Official long-run projections are 1.2–1.3% a year. [INE](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/CNA2025.htm) · [CaixaBank Research](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/es/node/13370/printable/print)
- **So far:** Weakened
- **By 2050:** In doubt
- **Spread:** 5 items: 1 invalidated, 2 weakened, 1 strengthened, 1 confirmed.

**4. Technology creates more jobs than it destroys**

- **The report assumed:** Technology is a net gain for jobs, and digital tools raise the odds of catching up. (Stated)
- **How much rests on it:** Five of nine chapters.
- **Evidence now:** Employment has grown in every group of jobs exposed to AI since 2022. The authors say it is too early for firm conclusions. [BBVA Research and Fedea](https://www.bbvaresearch.com/publicaciones/espana-ia-del-empleo-expuesto-a-la-productividad/)
- **So far:** Confirmed, provisionally
- **By 2050:** Too early to say
- **Spread:** 8 items: 2 confirmed, 1 strengthened, 1 weakened, 4 without evidence.

**5. The public spending envelope**

- **The report assumed:** Spending on pensions, health and care stays at or below 25% of GDP in 2050. (Stated)
- **How much rests on it:** Three of nine chapters.
- **Evidence now:** About 25.5% of GDP in 2050 on current policies. [CaixaBank Research, using AIReF](https://www.caixabankresearch.com/sites/default/files/content/file/2025/09/18/34411/im09_25_09_dossier_3_en_0.pdf) (secondary account)
- **So far:** Weakened
- **By 2050:** In doubt. The evidence here is itself a projection for 2050, so the two views are the same test.
- **Spread:** 9 items: 2 confirmed, 2 strengthened, 4 weakened, 1 mixed.

**6. Housing**

- **The report assumed:** House prices rise in line with inflation, and about 73,600 new households form each year. (Stated)
- **How much rests on it:** Three of nine chapters.
- **Evidence now:** Prices rose 12.2% in the year to the second quarter of 2026. About 219,000 households a year have formed since mid-2023, against 91,896 homes completed in 2025. [INE prices](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/IPV2T26.htm) · [INE population](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ECP2T26.htm)
- **So far:** Invalidated
- **By 2050:** Does not apply. The assumption has no 2050 value.
- **Spread:** 7 items: 1 invalidated, 1 weakened, 1 strengthened, 3 confirmed, 1 without evidence.

**7. Climate timing and the pace of transition:** Not yet reviewed.

**8. The eight-country benchmark:** Not yet reviewed. **[STRAIN]** Evidence from theme 3 bears on it: productivity in the benchmark countries grew only 0.6% a year in 2008–2025.

**9. Political delivery:** Not yet reviewed. **[STRAIN]** One of its two items was tested in the fiscal pass and rated weakened.

See all 102 items in the full register: [pilot register doc](https://claude.ai/artifact/NhRYA5RCxM5XtLTsuT6bk4) (private draft).

## 8. Where things stand against the scenarios

| Element | Closer to | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economic growth | Neither: above both | 2.4% to 3.7% a year in 2023–2025, against 0.3–1.1% and 1.5%. |
| Productivity | Baseline | 0.6% a year in 2022–2025, against the 1.5% the second path needs. |
| Jobs | Convergence | Unemployment at 10% in mid-2026, already below the 12% target for 2030. |
| Income per head | Convergence | Grew 0.99% a year in 2020–2025, against 0.26% in the benchmark countries. |
| Population | Neither: above both | Both scenarios share one population path. Spain passed its 2050 level in 2025. |
| Pension and ageing costs | Baseline | Pensions projected at 16.1–16.4% of GDP in 2050 (May 2026). |
| Tax revenue | Convergence on level, not on mix | 38.1% of GDP in 2025. Environmental taxes fell to 1.61% (2024). |
| Housing | Neither: harder than the baseline | The shortfall since 2021 is about 750,000 homes (June 2026). |

**Overall:** Spain is on neither of the report's two paths. It has grown faster than both by adding workers, mainly through migration, while output per hour has barely moved. Jobs and income are moving toward the second path; productivity, the variable the report made central, is not.

The report did not model this path.

**[STRAIN]** "Neither" needed a direction to mean anything. The pilot named the observed path "extensive growth" and proposed it as a third scenario. That name is left out here, following the proposed default for decision 4.

## 10. What the report did not cover

- **Topics not discussed that have since mattered:** A word search of the main text found no mention of defence spending, tariffs, protectionism, strategic autonomy, natural gas, semiconductors or disinformation. Inflation appears four times; interest rates, public debt and geopolitics once each. Generative AI is absent. The report names three open questions without modelling them: a rift between China and the United States, regional supply chains, and a stronger or weaker EU.
- **Major events since publication:** **[STRAIN]** The pilot holds no timeline. These were picked out of evidence rows, and several have no date.
  - Energy shock, 2022 (themes 2 and 5)
  - Net migration above 600,000 a year, 2022–2024 (themes 1, 3 and 6)
  - Pension reforms, 2021–2023 (theme 5)
  - Housing law, May 2023, and state housing plan, April 2026 (theme 6)
  - Trade shock from US tariffs, 2025 (theme 2)
  - Defence spending reaches 2% of GDP, 2025 (themes 2 and 5)
  - Middle East energy shock, 2026 (theme 2)
  - European recovery fund deadline, August 2026 (theme 9)

**[STRAIN]** Tone. The word search also found no mention of Ukraine. The report was published nine months before the invasion, so listing that as something it "does not discuss" reads as hindsight. It is left out of the list above.

## 14. How this review was done

- **What the AI did:** Read the report and logged 102 items. Ran a word search for absent topics. Grouped the items into nine themes and ranked them. Gathered evidence for six themes and proposed ratings.
- **What people checked:** One reviewer chose which themes to test first and set the source rule. **[GAP]** No rating has been reviewed, and there is no record that the implied assumption in theme 2 was validated.
- **Sources used:** INE, Banco de España, AIReF, Eurostat, the European Commission, OECD, IMF and Spanish ministries. BBVA Research, CaixaBank Research, Fedea, Funcas and Real Instituto Elcano.
- **Not reviewed:** Climate timing, the eight-country benchmark and political delivery. 64 of 102 items have no rating.
- **Limits:** **[STRAIN]** The pilot lists more than 20. The main ones: most figures come from automated extracts of source pages and were not checked line by line. AIReF's figures come through CaixaBank Research and indexed text. Care-system figures for mid-2026 and regional house prices come from secondary reports.
