## 1. At a glance

**Tip:** Spain has grown faster than both the report's paths by adding workers, but the productivity gap the report put at its centre has not narrowed.

(25 words.)

**Lead**

*España 2050* was written by the Spanish government's foresight office in May 2021 as the basis for a long-term national strategy. It aimed to close Spain's gap with eight leading EU economies by 2050. It set a path of reform against a baseline without it, and assumed slow population growth and a calm world outside. Five years on, Spain is on neither path. The economy grew between 2.4% and 3.7% a year in 2023–2025, faster than both, mostly by adding workers through migration. Output per hour is still about 74% of the benchmark level. The proposals on productivity, education, pensions, and care still stand; those on migration, tax, and housing are worth revisiting.

(112 words.)

**Three things to know**

1. Spain had 49.8 million residents on 1 July 2026, already above the 49.3 million the report used for 2050. *As of August 2026, INE.*
2. Output per hour has stayed at about 74% of the eight benchmark countries' level for 18 years. The gap the report put at its centre is still there. *As of October 2026, BBVA Research.* **[GAP]** Tier 2 source; verify at INE or Eurostat before this goes above the waterline.
3. House prices rose 12.2% in the year to the second quarter of 2026. The report expected them to rise in line with inflation. *As of September 2026, INE.*

## 2. How to read this page

**Overline:** 02 · How to read this page

**Action title:** A vision with a comparison path: the goal is an aim, not a forecast

This report is a vision with a comparison scenario. Its convergence path is the authors' goal for 2050. Its baseline projects the trends of 1980–2019 forward, to show what happens without reform. Neither is a forecast.

Where a figure is "behind", that describes the distance between the latest published number and the report's own target. It does not describe a government. The yardstick throughout is the report's own aims, not the reviewer's view of what is good.

Two readings are kept apart. A **rating** says what the evidence shows so far about something the report assumed. A **pressure reading** says what new conditions may mean from here. This page has no pressure readings yet, because no change scan has been run (§10).

| Shape | Public label | Register term | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrow along the full heading | Holds | Confirmed | The evidence matches what the report expected. |
| Arrow past the end, double head | Goes further | Strengthened | The variable has moved further in the direction the report expected. |
| Arrow stopping halfway | Holds in part | Weakened | The direction holds; the size or timing does not. |
| Arrow leaving at a wide angle | Not supported so far | Invalidated | The evidence so far runs against what the report expected. |
| Dotted heading, open origin | Not yet reviewed | Not yet reviewed | Not yet testable, or not yet checked. |

| Shape | Target position | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dot above the path | Ahead | The latest figure is past the report's target for its year. |
| Dot on the path | On path | Level with it. |
| Dot below the path | Behind | Short of it. |
| Dot inside a ring at the path's end | Met | The target has been reached. |

Where lower is better (energy poverty, housing cost overburden), "ahead" still sits above the line.

What this report allows us to test: it gives figures for both paths and dates most of its targets, so we compare them with published statistics. Ratings rest on official sources only: statistics offices, central banks, ministries, and international bodies. Think tanks give context. News reports never support a rating.

## 3. Why it was written

**Overline:** 03 · Why it was written

**Action title:** Written during the pandemic, it set out to close Spain's gap with Europe's leaders

- **Who asked for it:** **[GAP]**
- **Who wrote it:** The Oficina Nacional de Prospectiva y Estrategia, the government's foresight office. The report lists its contributors and draws on about 1,650 publications and 500 data series. **[GAP]** The number and names of contributors were not recorded.
- **The moment:** Written during the COVID-19 pandemic, which the report treats as a passing shock with recovery by 2022–2023. - **How it was made:** The 27 EU states were sorted into low, medium and high performers. Spain's past 30 to 40 years were reviewed and projected to 2050 as a baseline. A second path was drawn in which reforms close the gap with the high group. A roadmap of 50 targets and more than 200 proposals works back from that path.

## 5. The futures it set out

**Overline:** 05 · The futures it set out

**Action title:** A goal of convergence by 2050, set against a baseline without reform

Every chapter sets the same two futures side by side. The second is a projection, drawn as a warning; the first is the goal the 50 targets work back from.

**Card 1.** Label: THE REPORT'S GOAL

- **Name:** Convergence
- **The story:** Reforms close or narrow the gap with Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden. A cross-party agreement carries the strategy through several legislatures.
- **In 2050:** Productivity 50% higher · employment rate 80% · growth 1.5% a year · tax revenue 43% of GDP · all electricity renewable.
- **What it needs:** More than 200 proposals delivered. An agreement that lasts several legislatures. Supportive EU funds and climate policy.

**Card 2.** Label: COMPARISON PATH

- **Name:** Baseline
- **The story:** Spain carries on as it did from 1980 to 2019, with no major reforms. A shrinking workforce slows the economy and strains the welfare state.
- **In 2050:** Growth 0.3–1.1% a year · 3.7 million fewer people aged 16 to 64 · pension spending 15.2–16.9% of GDP.
- **What it assumes:** No major policy change. Net migration of about 197,000 a year. No lasting shocks.

**How the futures relate:** Both share one population path and one outside world. Policy is the only thing that separates them.

### 7.1 Spain passed the report's 2050 population in 2025

- **In the report (2021):** 49.3 million people in 2050, with net migration averaging 197,000 a year. Stated.
- **How much rests on it:** Seven of nine chapters.
- **Now:** 49,801,559 residents on 1 July 2026. Net migration was 727,005 in 2022, 642,296 in 2023, and 626,268 in 2024. *INE, population at 1 July 2026, as of August 2026; INE, migration statistics 2024, as of December 2025.*
- **Rating so far:** Not supported so far (register: invalidated)
- **So what:** Workforce, housing need, and pension costs in seven chapters start from a smaller population than Spain now has.
- **Spread:** 8 items: 2 not supported so far, 4 hold in part, 1 goes further, 1 holds.

### 7.2 Trade, energy, and defence shocks have arrived that the report did not expect

- **In the report (2021):** Open trade, stable energy supply, low inflation and interest rates, and no major new claims on public spending. Implied by the report's argument.
- **How much rests on it:** All nine chapters.
- **Now:** High US tariffs from 2025. Energy shocks in 2022 and again in 2026. Inflation forecast at 3.6% for 2026. Defence spending at 2% of GDP since 2025. *Banco de España, annual report 2025, as of June 2026; BBVA Research, defence spending.* **[GAP]** The defence figure comes from a Tier 2 source; verify at Tier 1.
- **Rating so far:** Not supported so far (register: invalidated)
- **So what:** Every chapter's spending and growth paths were set for a calmer world than the one Spain now faces.
- **Spread:** 6 items: 1 not supported so far, 2 hold in part, 1 goes further, 2 hold.
- **[GAP]** No record that a person validated this implied assumption (method decision 3).

### 7.3 Growth has run above both paths, from more workers rather than higher productivity

- **In the report (2021):** Without reform, growth of 0.3–1.1% a year from 2023 as the workforce shrinks. With reform, productivity 50% higher by 2050. Stated.
- **How much rests on it:** Five of nine chapters.
- **Now:** GDP grew 2.4% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024, and 2.6% in 2025, mostly by adding workers. Output per hour grew 0.6% a year in 2022–2025. Long-run projections are 1.2–1.3% a year. *INE, national accounts 2023–2025, as of September 2026; BBVA Research, October 2026; CaixaBank Research.* **[GAP]** The productivity and long-run figures come from Tier 2 sources; verify at INE and the official projection.
- **Rating so far:** Holds in part (register: weakened)
- **So what:** The shrinking workforce the report planned around has not arrived, but the productivity gap it set out to close is still there.
- **Spread:** 5 items: 1 not supported so far, 2 hold in part, 1 goes further, 1 holds.

### 7.4 Jobs have grown in every AI-exposed group so far, as the report expected

- **In the report (2021):** Technology is a net gain for jobs, and digital tools raise the odds of catching up. Stated.
- **How much rests on it:** Five of nine chapters.
- **Now:** Employment has grown in every group of jobs exposed to AI since 2022. The study's authors say it is too early for firm conclusions. *BBVA Research and Fedea, AI and employment, as of September 2026.*
- **Rating so far:** Holds, provisionally (register: confirmed, provisionally)
- **So what:** The report's case for digital investment rests on this; the evidence so far is early.
- **Spread:** 8 items: 2 hold, 1 goes further, 1 holds in part, 4 without evidence.
- **[GAP]** The rating rests on a Tier 2 source only. Under the evidence rules it cannot stand until a Tier 1 source is found. **[STRAIN]** "Provisionally" is a qualifier the scale does not have (template-test break 1).

### 7.5 Ageing costs are projected just above the report's 25% ceiling

- **In the report (2021):** Spending on pensions, health, and care stays at or below 25% of GDP in 2050. Stated.
- **How much rests on it:** Three of nine chapters.
- **Now:** About 25.5% of GDP in 2050 on current policies. *CaixaBank Research, using AIReF, as of September 2025.* **[GAP]** Read through a Tier 2 carrier; verify at AIReF.

## Notes from the rebuild

Points the brand system raised that the spec and the text alone did not. Each is a proposal.

1. **The tip rests on a Tier 2 figure.** The productivity gap is the report's central diagnosis and the page's headline finding, but its figure comes from BBVA Research. Before publication, either verify it at INE or Eurostat, or move a Tier 1 finding into the tip.
2. **Public labels lose the qualifiers.** "Holds, provisionally" and the split ratings in the register have no marker. The labels need either a qualifier slot in the marker row or method fix 1 (two questions per item).
3. **"Goes further" on opposite readings.** The same migration figure gives "not supported so far" (population) and "goes further" (the migration sensitivity test). On a page with bearing markers the contradiction is more visible, not less, because the two arrows point different ways.
4. **Scenario cards on a hybrid.** The design-system preview gives the goal an Ember band and the baseline a Bloom band. Brand §4 keeps Ember out of data and scenario inks off vision pages. This draft uses Loam for both. Decide which rule leads.
5. **Target positions need a path.** The target-position marker means "against the path for this year". The pilot only holds 2030 end-points, so "ahead" means "already past the 2030 level". Either draw paths in the evidence pass or add a marker for "past the target date's level early".
6. **No call to action.** With no download, citation, or error channel, nothing earns the page's one Ember button. The first candidate is "Download the register".
7. **Limits sit better on their figures.** In the laid-out page each limit appears as a note beside the figure it touches, and §15 keeps the table. This makes spec v0.2's single limits log redundant on screen.
8. **Section 6 and section 7 still repeat** (spec open question 7). In the laid-out page §6 works as a one-screen summary for phones; §7 carries the evidence.
